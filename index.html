Trellis

Something to grow on.

A measured CSS and HTML framework for agents and humans working together.

Trellis helps you grow a design system into a product. Choose a few things with care, and let everything else follow. It holds your core choices and lets them be expressed many ways without losing their harmony.

What it is

Relational

Nothing has a value of its own. Sizes, spaces and colors are written as relationships to your core choices.

Proportional

Cells that share one measure, and ratios that open with the space, make harmonies you can feel before you can name them.

Rhythmic

Blocks land on lines at regular intervals. You set the tempo and the reader can change it. Regularity is what lets the expressive dance off the page.

Responsive

The grid answers to the screen, type flows to fit its container, and both follow the reader's settings, so your content reaches all eyes with equal care.

Fractal

The same structure repeats at every scale. Any card can be the context for what it contains, so the markup never has to change.

Tunable

Play with proportions and parameters until it feels right and works the way you need.

Legible

On the surface and beneath it. Classes and markup are named to be read by people and by agents. A measured vocabulary makes a measured interface.

Checkable

A test fails when an edge leaves its line.

Portable

One token file and one math module for every platform.

Collaborative

Shared roots and clear boundaries let many hands work at once.

Use the bar above to switch theme and cell size, and turn on Measure to see every space on this page named in cells.

Elements

Point, line, plane +

Digital interfaces are made of points, lines and planes. On a screen, every pixel and every line is really a plane, however thin. Trellis is built on that understanding, and grows from it: screens are made of pixels, but the principles behind its choices are geometric. With depth and time, the plane extrudes into a solid, moves, and becomes another form by rule.

Point

A drawn dot is half a cell across, and the hairline is one device pixel. Neither ever scales.

Line

One CSS pixel is three device pixels across on a modern phone, nine in all. Lines are drawn in device pixels, aware of the screen they land on.

Plane

The cell, and every surface built from cells: cards, panels, sections, the page.

+

Solid

The plane, extruded. A cube is six planes, twelve lines and eight points: everything above, in three dimensions, drawn at the lattice's 2:1 slope.

Motion

The cube makes a quarter turn on one axis, rests, then turns on the next. Each turn starts and ends on the lattice. The layout never moves, and the cube holds still for readers who ask for less motion.

Cube

Transformation

One form becomes another by moving its points. The cube's corners are cut until it becomes the cuboctahedron, Fuller's vector equilibrium, then the octahedron, and back again. Each form is scaled to keep the same size.

The unit

It halves and doubles

The unit is 0.75rem, so it follows the reader's font-size setting. On a wide screen it's rounded to an even pixel, so every half unit is whole. On a touch screen, in either orientation, it's fitted instead: an even number of cells spans the screen exactly, whole modules of four between the gutters, and the cell stretches by a percent or two to fill it. Lines never stretch: a hairline is one device pixel. Its target is 10px on a phone, rising to 12px on a wide screen. Step the cell in the bar above and every space, control, corner and avatar on this page follows.

Type on the cell

      
Headlines stand, text hangs

      
The cell is the only line on the page. Headlines are large enough to be seen keeping it: their line height rounds to whole cells and every baseline lands on a cell line. Smaller text runs on its own proportional line, which reads better than any grid could make it. It hangs from a cell line by the top of its capitals, and its block rounds up to whole cells, so whatever comes next starts on a line.

Color

      
A few seeds, and every color follows

      
A product chooses seeds: one neutral, an accent, one per kind and one per status, each one color in its light form. Every role is a seed at a lightness, written with relative colors, so one declaration carries both themes, and the dark theme lifts each hue by --color-lift. The ink on a fill is computed from the fill. Everything else is a mix of a role into a ground.

Layout

      
Page, section, composition, surface, element

      
Five levels, each with a few named parts. A page holds sections; a section holds compositions; a composition arranges surfaces and elements. Every part starts on the cells, spaces blocks in whole cells and asks its container, not the window, how much room it has. A unique design changes variables at any level; it doesn't invent a sixth.

What the seed library taught us about tending

        
Three winters ago a dozen neighbors began trading seed in a borrowed church hall, with no plan beyond not losing the beans. The library now holds 1,140 varieties and has outlasted two of the groups that founded it. This is what we learned about keeping a shared thing alive, written down for the next group that tries.

        
None of it is original. Most of it we relearned from the people who kept seed before us, and the rest we found by getting it wrong, usually in stratification season, when a mistake takes a full year to show.

        
        
Start with what can be lost

        
A library of books survives neglect. A library of seed does not: germination falls every year on the shelf, so the collection is always quietly dying, and the only cure is to plant it out. We keep a simple rule on the wall: anything older than its viability, multiplied by 2⁄3, goes in the ground this season. In chemical terms the shelf is fighting H2O and O2, so jars and silica matter more than labels.

The log, and why it is boring

        
Every jar has a line in one file. We resisted anything cleverer for years. To add a variety, press Ctrl + N in the sheet, or use the small script below, which refuses a line with no grower's name.

        
function addVariety(log, seed) {
  if (!seed.grower) throw new Error('Who grew it? A seed with no story gets lost.');
  log.push({ ...seed, added: new Date().toISOString(), generation: seed.generation ?? 1 });
  return log;
}

A note on the numbers

        
These are ours, in our climate, in glass, in a cool room. Yours will differ, which is the reason to run your own tests. A test takes ten seeds and a wet paper towel.

        
Reading a germination test

        
Count at seven days and again at fourteen. The second count is the honest one.

        
Fine print

        
Figures are rounded. No seed was harmed beyond the usual.

        

        
A season at the library

        
The swap is the part people see. It takes one Saturday; the rest of the year is what makes it possible.

        
        
        
        

          What if a variety fails two years running?
          
We ask whoever brought it first. If no one is left to ask, it goes to the regional bank with its whole log, and we write down why it failed here so the next grower starts ahead of us.

        

        

          Can I take seed without bringing any?
          
Yes. The first year is a gift. After that we ask you to return a tenth of what you grow, and we'll show you how to dry it.

A post type, worked through

        
An article, with a cover and its people

        
A .cover, then a .split holding .prose and a .list of related posts, then a .list of comments. No layout here that isn't one of those.

      

    

    

      

        
The cover is the part people remember, and it reaches the screen's edges. The words don't: they stay in the field, on the same left edge as everything below them.

        
The article is plain .prose. It knows nothing about the cover above it or the related posts beside it, so the same content can appear in a feed, an email or a course without changing.

        
Why the sidebar is a list

        
Because a related post, a comment and a chat are the same thing to the grid: a row with something at its start, words in the middle and something at its end.

Edges

      
Photo on the cells, halo in the gutter

      
A box's edges land on lines, or a hairline or quarter unit off them. A box centered on a line is then an even size. A box pinned to something else, like the edge of the screen, takes whatever size lands its free edge on a line. The avatar shows it best: the photo is whole cells, and its gap and ring are drawn outside it by the same geometry, so they stay even all the way round.

Always a ring
At every size. It carries the most specific color the app knows: the person's own color where the app gives people one, the person or group kind color where it doesn't. It never shows status, role or selection.

        
Always a gap
1px gap and 2px ring up to 3u; a quarter unit each from 4u. Together they are a quarter or a half unit, so the ring's outer edge lands on a line too.

        
Shape is kind
A circle is a person. A rounded square, corner a quarter of its size, is a group: a circle, an organization or a team. Shape never shows size, rank, role or context.

        
No photo
Initials on a wash of the ring color: one letter up to 2u, two from 3u. Never a placeholder image, and nobody dropped from a list.

        
Sizes by place
u inline in text · 2u rows, chips and stacks · 3u cards and messages · 4u headers · 6u profile cards · 8u page headers · 12u the profile page.

        
Presence and stacks
Presence is a ½u dot on the circle's diagonal, only in live places. Stacks overlap by half a unit, knock out with the surface, and always end in a count.

Organic

      
Grow from the grid

      
A community map, a constellation of people, a field of work and a web of relationships don't sit on a grid, and shouldn't. Their rules branch from the same seed. Positions may be free or sit on a lattice; sizes, strokes and labels still come from the unit, the octaves and the cells. This lattice slopes 2:1, so every lattice point is also a cell corner: the square grid is included, not left behind.

    

    

      

      

        
Kandinsky
Point, line, plane. The dot and hairline, the edge of a cell, the surface. Every mark is one of the three.

        
Mondrian
A few proportions in asymmetric balance, with the empty field doing work. Columns favor 1:2 over equal halves; space is a component, not leftovers.

        
Frank Lloyd Wright
The unit system: plans drawn on one module, the whole grown from the part. Organic means part and whole share one principle. That's u.

        
Buckminster Fuller
Triangulation and growth by frequency. The isometric lattice for maps and constellations; detail added by doubling, never by new numbers.

        
Christopher Alexander
Levels of scale, strong centers, boundaries. Neighboring sizes differ by about two, every element has a center, and a boundary, like a ring or a border, is proportional to what it bounds.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Free layouts

      
Free, flowing, moving, and still on the cells

      
Open, animated and organic layouts stay on the trellis by six rules. Each will get a demonstration of its own; the solids at the top of the page are the first, and the drawing above is the second.

    

    

      

        
Rest on the lattice, move off it
Layout places things on cells. Every motion starts and ends at a rest position on the lattice.

        
Time has a ratio too
Durations come from one small set and three easings: enter, exit, move. With reduced motion they collapse, and the rest state is complete on its own.

        
Free things live in layers
An organic or animated element sits in its own layer (.cells, a canvas, a positioned layer), its coordinates in cells. The flow around it never depends on it.

        
Spans, not pixels
A creative arrangement is written in cells or modules and re-placed by container queries, never by window width.

        
Content in the field, color to the edge
Text and controls stay in the field. A band's surface, a photograph or a rail may reach the screen's edges.

        
Scroll is a timeline
Scroll scenes use CSS scroll-driven animation, not scroll listeners, so they stay smooth and stop off-screen.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Keeping it

      
Rules a machine checks

      
An interface drifts when nothing stops the drift. Each of these is meant to fail the build, so a person or an agent hears about it before review. The tests in tests/ run in CI today; the rest come as lints, and until then these five are the pull request checklist.

    

    

      

        
No raw values. A color, size, space, radius, shadow or z-index outside the token layer fails.

        
Edges land on lines. A test checks every headline's baseline, every text block's top and every box's edges against the cells, in Chrome and in WebKit; a pixel test checks card edges against their grid lines' device pixels.

        
Every class is registered. A class the stylesheet doesn't define, or the style guide doesn't show, fails. That stops a new avatar before it starts.

        
Products turn knobs. A product layer may only set variables on the root. Variants and contexts set the primitive's own variables.

        
Inline styles set variables only. style="--av-hue: 30" passes; style="padding: 13px" fails.
