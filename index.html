Trellis

Something to grow on.

A CSS and HTML framework for people and AI agents building interfaces together.

You set a few values (the cell size, the type ratio, an accent color) and Trellis works out spacing, type sizes and colors from them. Each product changes those values to get its own look, and the markup stays the same.

One measure for every gap, inset, control and corner. A thing is so many cells wide, and its neighbor so many cells away. The screen width and the reader's text size set the cell, and everything measured in cells resizes with it.

What is Trellis?

Relational

Nothing has a value of its own. Sizes, spaces and colors are written as relationships to your core choices.

Proportional

Spacing shares one unit and type shares one ratio, so sizes relate to each other in fixed steps.

Rhythmic

Blocks land on lines at regular intervals. You set the interval and the reader can change it.

Responsive

The grid answers to the screen, type flows to fit its container, and both respect the reader's text size and reduced-motion settings.

Fractal

The same structure repeats at every scale. Any card can be the context for what it contains, so the markup never has to change.

Legible

Classes and markup are named to be read by people and by agents.

Checkable

A test fails when an edge leaves its line.

Portable

One token file and one math module for every platform.

Use the bar above to switch theme and cell size, and turn on Measure to see every space on this page named in cells.

Elements

Point, line, plane +

Digital interfaces are made of points, lines and planes. On a screen, every pixel and every line is really a plane, however thin. Trellis sizes things in those three geometric terms, even though the screen draws them in pixels. The demos below add depth and time: a solid, a motion and a transformation.

Point

A drawn dot is half a cell across, and the hairline is one device pixel. Neither ever scales.

Line

One CSS pixel is three device pixels across on a modern phone, nine in all. Lines are drawn in device pixels, aware of the screen they land on.

Plane

The cell, and every surface built from cells: cards, panels, sections, the page.

+

Solid

The plane, extruded. A cube is six planes, twelve lines and eight points: everything above, in three dimensions, drawn at the lattice's 2:1 slope.

Motion

The cube makes a quarter turn on one axis, rests, then turns on the next. Each turn starts and ends on the lattice. The layout never moves, and the cube holds still for readers who ask for less motion.

Cube

Transformation

One form becomes another by moving its points. The cube's corners are cut until it becomes the cuboctahedron, Fuller's vector equilibrium, then the octahedron, and back again. Each form is scaled to keep the same size.

The cell

Space is counted in cells

A space is a whole count of cells: one, two, three, five, as many as the design asks for. Halves and quarters live inside things. The cell's target is 10px on a phone, rising to 0.75rem (12px) on a wide screen, and it follows the reader's text size. On a wide screen it rounds to an even pixel, so half a cell is whole. On a touch screen it is fitted: an even number of cells spans the screen exactly. Step the cell in the bar above and every count here follows.

Lines

Lines are measured in pixels and don't change with the cell size. A line fills the pixels just after its coordinate, so a row of n cells drawn with borders takes n cells plus one line.

Line

--line

One CSS pixel, which is three device pixels on a 3× phone. Borders, rings, and the edges of the cells drawn here.

Hair

--hair

One device pixel, the thinnest mark a screen can make. The grid on this page, and the rule between rows. On a 1× screen it is the same as a line.

Counts and ratios

Counts measure space and a ratio measures type. They can agree. A product may favor a set of counts, as it chooses a ratio. Two sets, as examples:

Doubling

124816

Each count is twice the one before.

Fibonacci

1235813

Each count is the sum of the two before it, and each step comes closer to 1.618. It is the natural partner of a golden type ratio: margins, gaps and type then share one proportion.

Trellis enforces neither. Any whole count is on the grid.

The ratio

The type scale and its ratio

One ratio measures type. Headings step down from H1 by it, and none comes out smaller than body text: one the ratio would put under it takes body's size. The text sizes step out from body by half a step, its square root. Each size has one value for a narrow container and one for a wide container, and scales linearly between them.

A narrow space shrinks the step and keeps every role's place: H1 sits as many steps above body there as in a wide space, so the hierarchy compresses without losing its shape.

Seven values set all of it: three sizes, two ratios and two spaces. Drag them, or start from a named ratio. Everything else is worked out, and shown beside its sum. The two columns of samples are set by the ratio alone, each line on its own line height, so a size moves as its slider does; on a real page, headlines snap to the cell lines and smaller text hangs from them, as the box under the graph shows.

Type on the cell

      
How headlines and text sit on the cells

      
Headlines are big enough that a reader notices whether they line up, so their line height rounds to whole cells and each baseline sits on a cell line. Smaller text runs on its own proportional line, which reads better than any grid could make it. It hangs from a cell line by the top of its capitals, and its block rounds up to whole cells, so whatever comes next starts on a line.

Color

      
Color seeds and roles

      
A product chooses one seed, the accent, in its light form. The others follow from it, the neutral, a seed for each kind, one for each status and one for each of seven stages, and a product may set any of them in its place. Every role is a seed at a lightness, written with relative colors, so one declaration carries both themes, and the dark theme lifts each hue by --color-lift. The ink on a fill is computed from the fill. Everything else is a mix of a role into a ground.

    

    

      

        
Start from one color

        
You can start a palette from the accent alone. Core turns it around the wheel for the five kinds, a fifth apart; the three statuses keep their own hues and take its strength; the neutral takes a trace of its hue. Each takes the accent's lightness, kept clear of where ink flips from white to dark, so its ink reads at 4.5:1 at any hue, and no more chroma than sRGB holds there. Every role in both themes then follows as before. Core handles the Even harmony and the accent-hued neutral itself. The other harmonies and neutrals write out their own seeds.

Layout

      
Page, section, composition, surface, element

      
Five levels, each with a few named parts. A page holds sections; a section holds compositions; a composition arranges surfaces and elements. Every part starts on the cells, spaces blocks in whole cells and asks its container, not the window, how much room it has. A custom design changes variables at any of these five levels rather than adding a new one.

What the seed library taught us about tending

        
Three winters ago a dozen neighbors began trading seed in a borrowed church hall, with no plan beyond not losing the beans. The library now holds 1,140 varieties and has outlasted two of the groups that founded it. This is what we learned about keeping a shared thing alive, written down for the next group that tries.

        
None of it is original. Most of it we relearned from the people who kept seed before us, and the rest we found by getting it wrong, usually in stratification season, when a mistake takes a full year to show.

        
        
Start with what can be lost

        
A library of books survives neglect. A library of seed does not: germination falls every year on the shelf, so the collection is always quietly dying, and the only cure is to plant it out. We keep a simple rule on the wall: anything older than its viability, multiplied by 2⁄3, goes in the ground this season. In chemical terms the shelf is fighting H2O and O2, so jars and silica matter more than labels.

The log, and why it is boring

        
Every jar has a line in one file. We resisted anything cleverer for years. To add a variety, press Ctrl + N in the sheet, or use the small script below, which refuses a line with no grower's name.

        
function addVariety(log, seed) {
  if (!seed.grower) throw new Error('Who grew it? A seed with no story gets lost.');
  log.push({ ...seed, added: new Date().toISOString(), generation: seed.generation ?? 1 });
  return log;
}

A note on the numbers

        
These are ours, in our climate, in glass, in a cool room. Yours will differ, which is the reason to run your own tests. A test takes ten seeds and a wet paper towel.

        
Reading a germination test

        
Count at seven days and again at fourteen. The second count is the honest one.

        
Fine print

Floating surfaces

      
Popovers, menus and tooltips

      
Every popover, menu and tooltip is the same surface with different variables. It opens in the browser's top layer, follows its anchor and closes when the anchor scrolls out of view. Without that, one menu follows its button out of the card, one is clipped inside it, and the rest drift when anything scrolls.

Scrollbars

      
The scrollbar and its two variants

      
Every scroll area uses one archetype: a width, a thumb, a track. Each variant changes one variable and is defined in core, so a new large pane uses .cc-scroll-thick rather than a new style. The archetype can be redesigned later in one place.

Organic

      
Maps and constellations on the lattice

      
A community map, a constellation of people, a field of work and a web of relationships don't sit on the square grid. Their positions can be free or on a lattice, but sizes, strokes and labels still come from the cell: counts of cells, and the line. This lattice slopes 2:1, so every lattice point is also a cell corner, and the square grid is part of it.

    

    

      

      

        
Kandinsky
Point, line, plane. The dot and hairline, the edge of a cell, the surface. Every mark is one of the three.

        
Mondrian
A few proportions in asymmetric balance, with empty space as part of the composition. Columns favor 1:2 over equal halves, and empty space is planned like any other part.

        
Frank Lloyd Wright
The unit system: plans drawn on one module, the whole grown from the part. Organic means part and whole share one principle. In Trellis that module is the cell, --u.

        
Buckminster Fuller
Triangulation and growth by frequency. The isometric lattice for maps and constellations; detail added by more of the same part, never by new numbers.

        
Christopher Alexander
Levels of scale, strong centers, boundaries. Neighboring sizes differ by about two, every element has a center, and a boundary, like a ring or a border, is proportional to what it bounds.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Free layouts

      
Six rules for free and animated layouts

      
These six rules keep open, animated and organic layouts on the cells. Each will get a demonstration of its own; the solids at the top of the page are the first, and the drawing above is the second.

    

    

      

        
Rest on the lattice, move off it
Layout places things on cells. Every motion starts and ends at a rest position on the lattice.

        
Time has a ratio too
Durations come from one small set and three easings: enter, exit, move. With reduced motion they collapse, and the rest state is complete on its own.

        
Free things live in layers
An organic or animated element sits in its own layer (.cells, a canvas, a positioned layer), its coordinates in cells. The flow around it never depends on it.

        
Spans, not pixels
A creative arrangement is written in cells or modules and re-placed by container queries, never by window width.

        
Content in the field, color to the edge
Text and controls stay in the field. A band's surface, a photograph or a rail may reach the screen's edges.

        
Scroll is a timeline
Scroll scenes use CSS scroll-driven animation, not scroll listeners, so they stay smooth and stop off-screen.

      

    

  


  
  

    

      
Checks

      
Rules the build checks

      
Each rule below is meant to fail the build, so a person or an agent hears about it before review. The tests in tests/ run in CI today; the rest come as lints, and until then these five are the pull request checklist.

    

    

      

        
No raw values. A color, size, space, radius, shadow or z-index outside the token layer fails.

        
Edges land on lines. A test checks every headline's baseline, every text block's top and every box's edges against the cells, in Chrome and in WebKit; a pixel test checks card edges against their grid lines' device pixels.

        
Every class is registered. A class the stylesheet doesn't define, or the style guide doesn't show, fails. So a one-off avatar style can't be added without registering it.

        
Products turn knobs. A product layer may only set variables on the root. Variants and contexts set the primitive's own variables.

        
Inline styles set variables only. style="--face: var(--kind-person)" passes; style="padding: 13px" fails.
