# M3 · Inset

## What it is

Content sits whole cells inside a surface's edge. It is the padding of a surface, named as a relation so that a box and a word share one vocabulary.

## Its parts

- **The edge**, which is on a line (R3).
- **The padding**, in whole cells between blocks, so the first line of content inside starts on a line.
- Inside a control, padding is in half cells, and the control counts as one thing whose own edges stay on lines.

## What it owns

The space between a surface's edge and its content. Nothing outside the edge: that is Outset.

## How it meets the line

From inside: the content's start line is a whole number of cells in from the edge's line.

Example: a `.card` with the default padding of 2 cells. Its edge is on a line, and its first block of text starts two lines in. In the Almanac example layer the sides are 1 cell and the top and bottom 2, and both still land on lines.

Controlled by: the knobs `--density-card-pad`, `--density-card-pad-inline` and `--density-card-pad-block`; on a card, `--card-pad`, `--card-pad-inline` and `--card-pad-block`. A tag's padding is `--tag-pad`, equal to its corner. A list row's is `--list-pad-y`. A band's block padding is 6 cells.

## Rules it keeps

- R3: the edge is a plane after its coordinate.
- R6: between blocks, space is whole cells.
- The open item "Padding clears the corner" would add a floor on padding from the radius.
