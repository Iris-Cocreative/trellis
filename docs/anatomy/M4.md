# M4 · Outset

## What it is

A surface's edge steps whole cells outside the line its content keeps. A card with one cell of padding steps one cell out, so the text inside it lines up with the text beside it. Through 0.9 this was called Hang; the knob and the name changed in the release after 0.9.0, and the old knob has no alias.

## Its parts

- **A card in a stack or a section body** steps out `--outset` cells on both sides, as negative inline margin.
- **A row of nothing but cards** (`.cols`, `.grid`, `.split`, `.rail`) steps out as a whole. A card inside one column of a mixed row stays in its column.
- **A `.list`** steps out by `--list-outset`, and each row takes that much inline padding back, so its hover ground reaches past the text while the text stays on the column's edge.
- **The `pre` in `.prose`** steps out like a card.
- Nested, a card steps out into its parent's padding.

## What it owns

The step, in whole cells. Not the padding (that is Inset) and never the box's size.

## How it meets the line

From outside: the content keeps the column's line; the edge sits on a line further out.

Example: the specimen's Layout section, "`.card` steps out", and the rows of the list demo. Turn off the Outset control in the specimen's top bar to see every card fall back inside the column.

Controlled by: `--outset`, a product knob, 1 by default; 0 turns it off. On a list, `--list-outset`, which follows it.

## Rules it keeps

- R5: surfaces step out. Gutters are at least two cells, so a card that steps out never reaches the screen's edge. An ancestor with hidden overflow clips the step: give it `--outset: 0` or a cell of inline padding.
- R14: products set `--outset`; a variant or context never does.
