# M1 · Stand

## What it is

The last baseline sits on a cell line. Headlines stand: `.t-h1`, `.t-h2`, `.t-h3`, and `h1` to `h3` in `.prose`. They are large enough that a reader sees them keeping the line.

## Its parts

- **The line height**, rounded to whole cells: `round(nearest, 1em × --t-lh, --u)`.
- **The box**, trimmed to the capitals and the alphabetic baseline (`text-box`), then padded on top by the cap height rounded up to whole cells, less the cap height. So the last baseline is on a line, and every line above it is too.
- **Its own space after it**, proportional to its size (H1 0.33em, 0.11em more a step down), rounded up to whole cells (D56). In a stack it is the larger of that space and the gap, never the sum.

## What it owns

Nothing outside its box. Standing is how a heading places itself; the composition around it only gives it a start on a line.

## How it meets the line

From below: the baseline rests on the line, like a word on a ruled page.

Example: in the specimen's "Headlines stand, text hangs" section, the grid shows each headline's baseline on a cell line at every screen width and in every product layer.

Controlled by: `--h1-min`, `--h1-max`, `--rh-min`, `--rh-max` (the size and the heading ratio) and `--lh-h1`, `--lh-h-step` (the line ratio before rounding). All are knobs in `product.template.css`. `math.js` has it as `standPad()` and `lineHeight()`.

## Rules it keeps

- R1: edges land on lines.
- R6: between blocks, space is whole cells.
- R19: the line height follows the cell, which stops at its cap; above it the heading's line still rounds to whole cells.
