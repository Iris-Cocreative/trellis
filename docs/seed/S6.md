# S6 Point, line, plane

## What it is

Kandinsky's three elements, read as the three measures of the seed:

- **The point:** the hairline and the half-cell dot.
- **The line:** a cell's edge, rhythm in one direction.
- **The plane:** the cell, and every surface built from cells.

Beyond the three, an experiment carries them on: the **solid** (the plane extruded), **motion** (the solid turns) and **transformation** (one solid becomes another by moving its points).

## Why

A small vocabulary of marks means every mark on the page is one of three, and each is sized from the seed. A reviewer can ask of any mark which of the three it is and whether its size came from the cell.

The same vocabulary carries into work that is not gridded, such as a map, a constellation of people or a web of relationships. Node sizes come from the octaves, strokes from the line scale, labels stand on cell lines, and rings and gaps follow the avatar rule (README, Organic interfaces).

## How it is computed, in plain words

- A point is one device pixel (`--hair`), one logical pixel (`--line`), or a dot of half a cell.
- A line sits at a cell coordinate and occupies the pixels just after it ([S5](S5.md)).
- A plane is a whole number of cells, plus one line if its edges are drawn.
- The isometric lattice slopes 2:1, so every lattice point is also a cell corner (`--iso-dx`, `--iso-dy`).
- In the experiment, a cube is drawn at the lattice's 2:1 slope. Motion moves rest states, never layout (F1). Transformations move points: cube to dodecahedron through the pyritohedra, cube to tetrahedron, cube to octahedron through the cuboctahedron, and octahedron to icosahedron along the golden ratio.

## Where it lives in the code

- `core.css`: `--hair` and `--line` in the tokens layer; the dot (`.dot`, `.avatar-dot`); the lattice tokens `--iso-dx` and `--iso-dy`.
- `experiments/solids.js`: the solid, motion and transformation, as an experiment. It is not a package export and stays out of the core until a product needs it. Where it could go: loading and empty states, transitions between views, the organic layer.
