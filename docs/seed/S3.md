# S3 Response

## What it is

Both measures answer to the screen. The screen sets how many cells there are and how far the ratio opens. The reader's dial scales the cell, and with it every space.

A page meets the screen in one of two ways. **Whole**, where a window frames the page: the cell is an even number of pixels and the frame steps by modules. **Fitted**, where the screen's edge frames the page: whole cells span the screen exactly.

The page sits in a centered `.frame`: a field of whole 4-cell modules, with a gutter of whole cells on either side. The field's edges, and every fourth line in from them, are the strongest lines, so the structure is symmetric about the screen at any width.

## Why

A page should be one structure at every width, not a set of designs swapped at breakpoints. The structure stays, and the number of cells changes.

On a phone the screen's edge is the frame. If whole cells did not span it, the last column would be a sliver, and the grid would be off center. Fitting makes the center of the screen a line and keeps both edges on lines.

## How it is computed, in plain words

**Whole.** The cell is the target rounded to an even point. The frame is the largest whole number of modules that fits, at most 100 cells. The gutter is 4 percent of the width rounded down to two cells, between 2 and 4 cells, and it clears the safe areas. The margins take what is left.

**Fitted.** On touch screens at 1.5× and up (phones in either orientation, foldables, tablets):

- width = screen − one device pixel, because the last line needs its pixel ([S5](S5.md)).
- count = 2 × round(width ÷ (2 × target)). It is even, so the screen's center is a line.
- cell = width ÷ count, rounded down to 1/32 of a point and held between 0.8 and 1.25 of the target. The 1/32 keeps every half cell exact in the browser's 1/64-pixel layout grid. The clamp means a browser that gets the math wrong still lays out a usable page.

**The gutter parity rule.** The field between the gutters must be whole modules. With an even count, that holds when the gutter has the same parity as half the count: gutter = 2 + (count ÷ 2) mod 2. So 40 cells take a gutter of 2 (a field of 36, 9 modules) and 42 take 3 (also 36). Beside a notch, the gutter is the fewest cells that clear the safe area, plus one if the parity is wrong.

**Worked examples.** A 393-point phone at 3× fits 40 cells of 9.8125 with a gutter of 2. A 440-point phone fits 44 cells of about 9.97. An 852-point phone in landscape fits 74 cells of 11.5, since the target has grown by that width. The cell strays at most about 2.5 percent from its target.

**What does not fit.** A 1× screen never fits: a fractional cell would draw equal gaps as 5px and 6px. Desktop windows don't fit by default, or every space would breathe while the window is dragged. `data-unit-fit="on"` forces fitting, and `"off"` refuses it.

**The dial.** `--u-scale` multiplies the target. The specimen's dial runs from 9 to 18, where 12 is 100 percent.

## Where it lives in the code

- `core.css`: `--frame-max`, `--frame-step` and `--gutter` for the whole case, and the fitted block (`--fit-w`, `--cells`, `--u`, `--gutter-safe`, `--gutter-n`) behind `@supports` for typed division and a coarse pointer at 1.5×. The old `tan(atan2())` trick is not used: WebKit computes it wrongly with viewport units, which blanked every iPhone in 0.3.0.
- `settle.js` writes the exact `--dpr` and, where a classic scrollbar makes `100vw` too wide, `--fit-width`.
- `math.js`: `fit()`, `wideFrame()` and `gutterCells()`.
- Tests: `tests/math.js` checks the fit's count, cell and gutter and the wide frame against the browser across eight screens. `tests/contract.js` reproduces the worked examples in Node. `tests/align.js` runs phones in Chromium and WebKit.
