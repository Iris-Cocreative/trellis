# S1 The cell

## What it is

The measure of space. Every gap, inset, control, corner and avatar is made of cells. In CSS the cell is `--u`.

It halves and doubles. The home values are a line, a quarter cell, a half cell, a cell and two cells (`--line`, `--sp-q`, `--sp-h`, `--sp-1`, `--sp-2`). The multiples are 1½, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16 cells. Half and quarter cells live inside things, such as a control's padding or a tag's gap. Between blocks, space is whole cells (R6).

## Why

One measure for all space makes every distance a relationship to one number, not a value someone picked. Change the number and everything follows, in proportion.

It also gives the page one line to land on. The cell is the only line on the page: every edge and every block of text meets it (R1, R6). A second grid, such as the half-cell baseline Trellis had before 0.5.0, gives two lines to drift between.

## How it is computed, in plain words

**The target.** Ten points up to a 500-point screen, twelve points from a 1000-point screen on, and a straight line between. Then three things scale it:

- The reader's text size, up to a cap. Call the text scale k, where 1 is the default, and take the smaller of k and 1.5. That number multiplies both ends and the line's starting point. The screen's share of the line does not scale.
- The cap. Above a text scale of 1.5 the cell stops growing and only type grows. iOS and Android accessibility sizes reach about three times normal, which would turn a 40-cell phone into a 13-cell one. With the cap, a 393-point phone at 312% keeps 26 cells of about 15 points, and layouts reflow because their breakpoints are in rem (R19).
- The reader's dial, which multiplies the result.

As `math.js` writes it: target = clamp(10 × k, (10 − 500 × slope) × k + slope × screen, 12 × k) × dial, where slope = (12 − 10) ÷ 500 and k is the text scale up to 1.5.

**Whole, where a window frames the page.** The cell is the target rounded to the nearest even point, so a half cell is whole too.

**Fitted, where the screen's edge frames the page.** On a touch screen at 1.5× and up, whole cells must span the screen. The count is the even number nearest (width − one device pixel) ÷ target. The cell is (width − one device pixel) ÷ count, rounded down to 1/32 of a point and held between 0.8 and 1.25 of the target. A 393-point screen at 3× gives 40 cells of 9.8125. [S3](S3.md) has the rest.

**The quarter.** A quarter cell is rounded to a pixel. So where two quarters must add up to a half, the second one is the half minus the first (`--sp-q2`), which is exact at any cell.

## Where it lives in the code

- `tokens.json`, the `cell` group: `target-narrow` 10, `target-wide` 12, `screen-narrow` 500, `screen-wide` 1000 and `text-scale-max` 1.5, in points. The `space` group holds the octaves, in cells.
- `tools/build-tokens.js` writes `--u-base` from those values. With the cap, it is the smaller of the rem line and the same line at the cap in px. Text size doesn't move px, so this needs no typed division and holds in every browser.
- `core.css`: `--u-scale` is the dial, `--u-target` is the target times the dial, and `--u` rounds it to an even pixel. The fitted block, behind `@supports` for typed division, replaces `--u` on touch screens. `--u` and `--cells` are deliberately not registered: Chrome passes a registered length on at six significant digits, and WebKit computed a registered cell count as 0. `--u-px` is a registered copy, kept for convenience and never used for layout.
- `math.js`: `cellScale()`, `cellTarget()`, `wholeCell()` and `fit()`.
- `settle.js`: `Trellis.cell()` measures the cell from one hidden box a cell wide. WebKit computes a registered length on the root against the default text size whenever a page or app sets the root's font size, so `--u-px` is wrong there while `--u` is right.
- Tests: `tests/math.js` checks the module against the stylesheet in Chromium and WebKit. `tests/contract.js` checks that `math.js` carries `tokens.json`'s seed. `tests/align.js` with the `ax` flag loads the specimen at 312% text.
