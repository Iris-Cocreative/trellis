# D114. A scope that is always dark says so in its selector

## Context

The contract checks contrast for a layer's root and for each of its scopes, in both themes (D96). A scope may be dark on every page: a mood, with `data-theme="dark"` in its markup. Its colors are chosen for the night, and the check failed them by day, in a theme nobody can see them in: the scopes study's lodge had `--ok` at 2.86:1 on `--bg-raised-3` in the light theme, against 3. The owner chose that the check skips the light theme for such a scope (2026-11-24). What was open is how the check knows.

## Options considered

**A list of names in the test.** The test would have to be edited for every product, and a product's own check (`npx trellis-product`) runs in another repository.

**Reading the markup.** The layer is checked alone: a product's markup is often a component in another language, and a scope that is dark on the pages found may be light on one not found.

**A knob, or a comment, that says so** (`--scheme: dark`). The layer would claim it and nothing would hold it to the claim: the same scope on an element with no `data-theme` takes its colors by day, unchecked.

**A knob that makes it so,** with core reading it into `color-scheme`. A container style query or a value that inherits into every scope inside it; a new mechanism beside `data-theme`, which already does this.

**The rule's selector.** `[data-product="lodge"][data-theme="dark"]` is a rule the browser applies only to an element with that attribute, and core makes such an element dark whatever the page is.

## Decision

**A scope's rule may be `[data-product="part"][data-theme="dark"]`. Such a rule is held to its contrast floors in the dark theme alone. Every other scope is held to them in both.**

The selector is not a claim about the markup: it is the condition. Where the markup doesn't ask for the dark theme, the rule's colors are not applied, the element has what the root chose, and that is checked in both themes.

A layer may hold a plain rule for the same scope beside it. The plain rule is checked in both themes, and the dark one stands on it: root, then plain, then dark, in the dark theme.

Only a scope, and only dark. A root follows the reader's theme. A rule for `data-theme="light"` is refused as it was, since no scope that is always light has asked.

## Consequences

`tools/product.js` takes the selector in a layer's shape, and `scopesOf()` returns with each scope the themes it is seen in. `tests/contract.js` checks it on layers made to fail by day, at night and in neither, and that core makes a `[data-product][data-theme="dark"]` element dark. The Figma variables read the same rules; not one of their values moves.

`experiments/scopes/grove.css` says it of the lodge and passes. It is checked with `examples/` on every run, so it can't regress. The lantern room, shown at night too, holds by day and keeps its plain rule.

Scopes inside scopes are not composed by the check (D96): a scope inside an always dark one, with `data-theme="light"` of its own, carries the dark one's seeds into the day and is not checked there.
