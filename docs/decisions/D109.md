# D109. Checkbox, radio and switch are one archetype

## Context

Core had a switch (D59) and no checkbox or radio. A checkbox was drawn only inside `.prose` task lists, as the browser's own box with a margin. Apps built their own: the second app's outlines draw a native checkbox with `appearance: none`, 1.1em square, a 1.5px edge at a third of the ink, filled with the accent and ticked when done; its admin screens leave the browser's box, or tint it with `accent-color`; the first app hides the input and draws a 24px box, a 22px ring with a dot, or Webflow's box over it. Neither had a focus style on its box, and the outline's hit area was the box alone. No app had a mixed checkbox, and outlines with nested checklists need one: a parent whose children are partly done. Every switch in both apps is a `button[role=switch]`, which core's switch already is.

## Options considered

**Three primitives,** `.checkbox`, `.radio` and `.switch`, each with its own row, gap and states. Simple to read, and three copies of the same row, which drift.

**A custom box over a hidden input,** as the switch draws its track on the label's `::after` with the input visually hidden. It keeps every box drawn the same way, but a checkbox written by Markdown or an editor (`li > input`) has no label to draw on, the focus ring has to be copied onto the label, and the input's own states (`:indeterminate`, `:user-invalid`) are read through `:has()`.

**ARIA widgets,** `[role=checkbox][aria-checked]` on a button, which allow `aria-checked="mixed"` in markup. They give up the form (a value, `required`, reset), Space and a radio group's arrows, which the native input has for free.

**One archetype on the native input** (chosen): the box is the input itself, drawn with `appearance: none`, and its mark its `::before`.

**The switch as `.choice.cc-switch`,** a new spelling for the same thing. It would break nothing, and give one control two names.

## Decision

- One archetype, `.choice`: a row whole cells tall (two cells; `.cc-lg` three), a box at its start on half cells, half a cell before its words (`.choice-label`), whose capitals center on the box (rule 8). The variants change the box: a checkbox is a square a cell and a half across with a quarter-cell corner (`.cc-sm` one cell, `.cc-lg` two), a radio the same circle, a switch a track three cells long.
- **The box is the native input.** `label.choice > input[type=checkbox|radio] + .choice-label`, so Space, a radio group's arrows, the form, `:checked`, `:indeterminate`, `:disabled` and `:user-invalid` are the browser's, and the focus ring is the global one, on the box. A box with no words beside it (a check beside text the person edits) is `label.choice > input[aria-label]`, which keeps the row and the hit area.
- **States.** Checked fills the box with `--choice-on` (the accent) and marks it in `--choice-ink` (its ink): a check, or a dot a third of the radio. **Mixed** is the input's `indeterminate` property, set from script (in React, a ref), drawn as a bar on the same fill: HTML has no attribute for it, and `aria-checked` on a native checkbox is against ARIA in HTML. Invalid (`aria-invalid="true"`, or `:user-invalid` once touched) turns the edge to the alert. Disabled quiets the box and the words and drops hover and press. Hover and press are the shared state layer on the box (D58).
- **The hit area is the row.** The label takes the click on its words; on a coarse pointer it grows an invisible `--hit-min` around itself, as a button does, so a box alone is still a 44px target.
- **The switch is the archetype's third box.** `.switch` keeps both its markups and its variables; it now takes the archetype's row, gap, `--choice-on` and `--choice-ink` from one shared rule. `aria-checked="mixed"` holds its thumb half way, for a setting over a selection that differs. A `role=switch` has no mixed state in ARIA and is read as off, so its label or description says so; on a menu row (`menuitemcheckbox`) mixed is valid.
- **A prose task list is the same box.** Markdown's `li > input` and an editor's `li > label > input + div` both draw the checkbox a cell and a half across, centered on the first line's capitals. A checkbox in prose records a state, so `disabled` (as Markdown writes every one) keeps its full ink. A done item's own words take the tertiary ink; a list nested in it keeps the text's. No strike-through: an ancestor's line can't be kept off a nested list.
- A label that may wrap takes `.cap-center`: once it wraps, `settle.js` marks it, it hangs from the row's top line and rounds up, and the box stays on its first line's capitals, reaching above the row by half the difference.
- `tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and touch: each box's size on half cells at its row's start, the row whole cells, the capitals' middle on the box's; a wrapped label's box on its first line; the touch hit area of a row, a bare box and a small one; checked, mixed, a chosen radio, invalid and disabled; Space, a radio group's arrow, a click on the words, a tap, a disabled box that won't toggle, the focus ring; a mixed switch; a prose task's box on its first line, at full ink.

## Consequences

Apps drop their own boxes: an outline's checklist is `label.choice` with the box alone, and the second app's `.ol-check` keeps its markup and loses its CSS. A nested checklist's parent shows mixed by setting `indeterminate` when its children differ; the kit owns that script. A card that is a choice (a radio card) is a kit pattern on `.card` with `:has(:checked)`, until a second app asks.

Forced colors draw the box in system colors: the edge in CanvasText, checked and mixed in Highlight with the mark in HighlightText.

Not in the Figma template yet: the file carries `switch`; a `choice` component (checkbox and radio × state × size) is for the next round of `tools/figma-components.js`.
