# D92. A cluster at a surface's end keeps its spare space above it

## Context

D55 made the last cluster in a card, a modal body or a member tile round up to whole cells, so its row no longer overflowed into the surface's padding. The row was centered in what it gained. A dense card's buttons are 2½ cells tall, in a row of three: a quarter cell above them and a quarter below, so the space under the buttons was the card's one cell of padding and a quarter more, against one cell at the sides. Where `settle.js` does the rounding, in Safari, the whole half cell went under the row. The owner, reading the specimen with Measure on, asked why the bottom padding was too big.

## Options considered

Named while building it:

Rounding the row down again, as every other cluster does. D55 was made because that ate the padding.

Dense buttons at two cells. It fixes this row and loses the density's own button size; a row of 1½-cell tags at a surface's end has the same half cell to place.

The spare space above the row. The gap above is whole cells already and takes a half more; the edge under the row is the surface's padding exactly.

## Decision

A cluster that rounds up at a surface's end packs its rows at its end: what it gained is above them. With `calc-size()` that is `align-content: end`; without it, `settle.js` gives the cluster the space as padding at its start, where it gave it as margin at its end.

## Consequences

- Under the last row of a dense card is its padding, one cell, where it was a cell and a quarter (a cell and a half in Safari). Above the row is the gap and a half cell.
- A cluster whose row is whole cells already does not move.
- `tests/layout.js` measures the space under a dense card's buttons against its padding, in both engines.

The owner asked why the padding was too big; the build session found the cause and proposed this.
