# D60. An eyebrow, a lighter label, a field inset

## Context

`.t-label` was the only label role, serving both as a section's eyebrow and as a field label, at `--fw-label` 500, the weight buttons, tags, tabs and shell links share. The owner asked for an eyebrow (a larger label), a thinner label, and field labels that line up with the input's placeholder; a field label started at the input's outer edge, a cell left of its text.

## Options considered

Weighed at the time, on a decision card in the plan: A, all three, a `.t-eyebrow` role, a `--fw-label-text` knob for label text only, and a `--field-inset` the label and hint start at; B, the eyebrow only, with field labels in `.t-sm` at 500 and the same inset, and no knob split; C, rename, today's label becoming the eyebrow and a new thinner `.t-label` replacing it (breaking: every label changes meaning).

## Decision

A. `.t-eyebrow` is the label one paragraph step up (label × rp), hanging, in `--label-case` and `--label-tracking`. `--fw-label-text` (400) weighs `.t-label`, `.t-eyebrow` and a prose `th`; controls keep `--fw-label`. `.field` sets `--field-inset` to the input's own text inset, a cell, half a cell in `.cc-dense` or beside `.input.cc-sm`, and its label and hint pad their start by it. It follows the input's text, not its corner, so a pill input still lines up.

## Consequences

Additive classes and knobs. Labels are lighter, a visual change: a product that set `--fw-label` for its labels sets `--fw-label-text` too. The eyebrow joins every role list (core, `settle.js`, `math.js`, the specimen and the tests). The specimen's section openers are eyebrows.
