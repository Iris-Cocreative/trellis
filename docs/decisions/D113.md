# D113. Padding clears the corner

## Context

A card's padding is two knobs (D42), so a product may tighten its sides. Nothing tied either to the card's corner: with one cell of padding and a corner of five, a card's first word sat on the curve. The review notes asked for padding of at least about three tenths of the radius; the share, and what it means when the two paddings differ, were left to the work.

The geometry: content's corner, p in from one side and q from the other, is inside a corner of radius r when (r - p)² + (r - q)² ≤ r², or when p or q is at least r. With p = q, that is p ≥ r (1 - 1/√2) = 0.293 r. With q larger, p may be smaller: r - √(q (2r - q)), which is nothing once q reaches r.

## Options considered

**One share for each direction, whatever the other is** (0.293 r, or a round three tenths). One line, and always enough. But it raises paddings that already clear: a card with a corner of a cell and a half, two cells at its sides and none above and below (a check in `tests/layout.js`) has content that starts where the curve has ended, and a product with tight sides and a roomy top, which is what the two knobs are for, would be overruled from a corner of three and a half cells.

**The circle itself.** Exact, but the difference of two squares of lengths has no CSS function: `hypot()` only adds them, and `sqrt()` takes a number, so a length must first lose its unit, by typed division (which core uses only behind `@supports`, for the fit) or through `tan(atan2())`.

**The chord of the circle.** From 0.293 r both ways to no padding where the curve ends: 0.415 (r - q). A quarter circle bulges outward, so the chord is always on the safe side, by at most 0.08 r, and it is exact at both ends. Lengths only.

**Snapping the floor to the next whole cell**, `max(padding, round(up, floor, u))`. A padding of a cell and a half that clears a floor of 1.2 cells would become two.

**Growing the padding by the whole cells it lacks.** A whole-cell padding stays whole cells, so text keeps its line and its left edge; a padding that clears the corner is not touched.

**Where the floor applies.** In the knobs' defaults, a context that sets `--card-pad-inline` on a card would pass it by. In the padding alone, bleeding media and scrollers, which give back `--card-pad-*`, would stop short of the edge.

**Refusing a tight padding in the product check** (`tools/product.js`). It sees the product's knobs and not what a context sets on a card.

## Decision

**A card's padding in each direction is held to the smaller of 0.293 r and 0.415 (r - q), where q is the other padding, and grows by whole cells where it falls short.**

A card lays out `--card-inset-inline` and `--card-inset-block`: its own padding plus what it lacks. `--card-pad`, `--card-pad-inline` and `--card-pad-block` stay what a product or a context sets. Everything that read the padding reads the inset: a nested card's step out and corner, `.card-media`, `.card-scroll`, `.media` and each of its bleeds.

The radius counts up to eight cells, because a pill's radius (`--r-round`, 9,999px) is not its real corner, which the browser caps at half the box's shorter side.

The radius and the two paddings are compared as registered lengths, so `--card-radius: 0` and `--card-pad-block: 0` mean what they say; they are never laid out, since a registered length loses its last digits (D6).

## Consequences

Nothing moves at the defaults or in a shipped layer. Measured before and after, with every box on the page compared, at 1440 and 393 wide and at 312% text, in Chromium and WebKit: 0 of 54 cards' paddings and 0 of 3,550 boxes differ with core, with Quarry or with Almanac, and 0 of 7 cards and 0 of 70 boxes in the scopes study. In cells, top and bottom by sides: core 2 by 2 on a corner of 1.5; Quarry 1 by 1 on 0.25; Almanac 2 by 1 on 2; a nested card 1 by 1 on 0.5; a dense one 1 by 1 on 1. One cell of padding both ways clears corners up to 3.4 cells, two clear 6.8.

On a rounder card a nested card may also be raised (to two cells in a parent of corner five), so its text no longer stands on its parent's line; a board lane's head keeps `--board-card-pad` and its cards may not.

A padding that just clears the curve, inside the chord's 0.08 r, gains a cell it did not need: one cell by two on a corner of five is exactly on the circle, and lays out two by two.

`.modal-inner`, `.float` and `pre` are not cards and keep their own padding.

`tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and touch; it fails 20 ways without the floor.
