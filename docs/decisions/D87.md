# D87. A frame keeps a reserve: it gives up its far margin first

## Context

The specimen keeps a column of navigation in the margin before the frame. The frame was always centered, so as a window narrowed both margins shrank together and the column ended up over the frame, while the margin on the other side, which held nothing, was still there. The owner asked that the empty side give way first, and said a Trellis layout should handle this by itself.

## Options considered

Leaving it to the page: a script measures the margin and hides the column when it runs out. That is what the specimen did, and it is why the column overlapped: the frame never knew anything was there.

Moving the column into the frame, as a `.shell`. A shell is an app's layout, with the navigation inside the field. A reading page wants its field whole and its navigation in the margin.

A reserve on the frame. Whatever lives in a margin says how much it needs, and the frame keeps that clear.

## Decision

`.frame` takes `--frame-reserve-start` and `--frame-reserve-end`, lengths in whole cells, set on the frame or above it. The frame stays centered while both margins cover their reserves. When one no longer does, the frame keeps that margin at its reserve and gives up the other margin; when that is gone too, it gives up its own width, a module at a time. With no reserve nothing changes.

`math.js`'s `wideFrame()` takes `reserveStart` and `reserveEnd` and returns both margins. `--frame-shift` says how far a reserve has moved the frame off the screen's center, so a cover's media still reaches both edges of the screen.

## Consequences

- A frame with a reserve is not always centered, so the strongest lines are no longer always symmetric about the screen. They are symmetric about the frame, as before.
- A reserve is for a window: where the screen's edge is the frame (fitted, on touch), set none, or the cells no longer span the screen. The specimen sets its reserve only where a pointer hovers.
- What bleeds to the screen's edges from inside a frame has to know the shift. The cover does. A band and a list's rules paint by an outset that already reaches past either edge.
- The specimen's column of navigation asks for three cells (points) or fourteen (names), and never sits over the frame.
