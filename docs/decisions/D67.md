# D67. A cap-centered label that wraps hangs

## Context

R8 centers a label's capitals on the control beside it (`.cap-center`), which assumes one line. At 312% text a row label in the specimen's tag matrix wrapped to two lines, 3.35 cells tall, and everything after it went off its line. The specimen avoided it by stacking those rows; the rule had no answer of its own, and no test would catch one.

## Options considered

Weighed at the time, on a decision card in the plan: A, a wrapped label rounds its block up to whole cells, like hanging text, and stays centered; B, it stops centering and falls back to hanging from its top line; C, a cap-centered label never wraps and truncates sideways (R11).

## Decision

B. `settle.js` counts the lines of every `.cap-center` in every browser, from its fragments' rectangles, and marks one that wraps with `data-trellis-wrap`. Core then aligns it to its row's start and rounds its block up to whole cells: `calc-size()`, or `--settle-n` in Safari, where the runtime used to skip cap-centered text. One that fits on a line still centers on its control.

## Consequences

R8 is settled. `tests/align.js` checks a wrapped one as text, its top and its end on lines; `tests/layout.js` builds one that must wrap and one that must not, in both engines. The runtime now watches cap-centered text in every browser, not only Safari.
