# D105. A clamp is clipped from the top

## Context

Every app clamps a title or a description to two or three lines. The only way to get the ellipsis is `-webkit-line-clamp` on a `-webkit-box`, which hides the rest with `overflow: hidden`. A type role trims its box to its capitals and its last baseline, so that clip cuts the tail off every g, p and y. Collab OS's owed-cards bench met it on a card's title, and fixed it with a quarter cell of padding given back as margin (`.oc-clamp`); half a cell had shown the top of the next line.

Measuring in both engines found more than the brief had:

- **The role's own rounding shows the hidden line.** A role rounds its box up to whole cells. The clip follows the box, so the spare part of the last cell shows the tops of the next line. A plain clamp on a role is wrong in both directions at once.
- **Padding given back as margin moves the rhythm.** The box is rounded with its padding in it, then the margin is taken off: the block ends up short of whole cells by the padding.
- **Both engines still paint the lines after the clamp** (Chrome 151, Safari 26.4). Neither has the unprefixed `line-clamp`, which would not. So any clip that reaches under the baseline is a clip that can show the next line.
- **Chrome trims a clamp's box and Safari does not.** In Chrome a clamped `-webkit-box` is laid out as a block, so the role's trim applies. In Safari it stays a box and nothing is trimmed: the text starts about six pixels under its line and the block is a cell taller. A block inside a role is no different.
- **Where the box is trimmed, the hidden lines move up** by what was trimmed under the baseline. The next line's tall letters sit about half a line less a capital, plus a third of an em, under the last baseline. In body text an accent on a capital there is already inside the descenders' depth.

## Options considered

Padding and a negative margin, as the bench and `.chat-quote-text` have it. It breaks the rounding (above), and in Safari the box is untrimmed anyway.

`overflow: clip` with `overflow-clip-margin`. No layout cost, but Safari has no `overflow-clip-margin`, and the margin is measured from the rounded box, so it shows the next line.

A wrapper: the role outside, the clamp inside. Two elements for one utility, and Safari does not carry the trim into the inner box either.

Leaving Safari a plain clamp, off its line. Safari is every browser on an iPhone, and a title a cell lower than its neighbor in the next card is what the rhythm exists to prevent.

Trimming in script everywhere. Chrome needs none of it, and a foundation that is CSS first stays so where the browser can do the work.

## Decision

- `.u-clamp`, on a type role, with `--lines` (2). It sets the trim itself, so its geometry is known whatever it is on.
- **The clip is a `clip-path` measured from the box's top**: the lines (`--lines` less one line heights, and a capital), and a reach under the last baseline. Nothing is added to the box and nothing given back, so the box is the role's own, rounded as the role rounds it. The clip does not follow the box's height, so the rounding shows nothing.
- **The reach is `--clamp-reach`, .24em**: the depth of g, j, p and y in the system faces (.21em to .24em). It is bounded by where the next line's ascenders are: half a line less a capital plus .33em where the box is trimmed natively, and a line less a capital elsewhere. A face with deeper descenders raises `--clamp-reach`.
- **Where a clamp's box is left untrimmed, `settle.js` trims it.** When the page starts it measures once whether a clamped box is trimmed. If not, each clamp is settled: three hidden one-line blocks of the clamp's font and line height (whole, trimmed at the start, trimmed at the end) give what a block trims; the clamp is marked `data-trellis-clamp`; `--clamp-top` moves the text up through a `::before` with a negative margin; and `--clamp-h` is the box's height, the trimmed lines rounded as the role would (nearest for a headline, up for other text, as it is with no role). Trims are kept by font and measured again when fonts load or the page resizes. A marked clamp takes the same clip.
- **The gate is a measurement.** The CSS-only clip is offered where `calc-size()` is, since that is where the box is trimmed today; the script decides by measuring and overrides it, so an engine that gains one without the other is still right once the script has run.
- Without the script, Safari keeps a plain `overflow: hidden` clamp: the whole text, a few pixels low, in a box a little tall.
- `tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and at 3x: three roles and a three-line clamp. The box is whole cells and on its line. From a screenshot, black on white: the rows of ink under the last baseline are as many as under the first line's, and the cell under them is empty. It fails with the reach at zero (cut) and with the bound removed (the hidden line shows).

## Consequences

A clamped title and an unclamped one in the next card stand on the same lines, in both engines.

The limits are those of two lines sharing one strip of pixels. In a headline set tighter than about 1.15, the hidden line's ascenders are inside the descenders' depth, and the clip stops at them: a tail loses its tip. An accented capital at the start of the hidden line can show the top of its accent, under a pixel in body text and a few pixels under a tight heading. Both end when a browser stops painting lines after a clamp (`line-clamp`, unprefixed), which neither does yet.

`clip-path` makes a stacking context and clips anything drawn outside it, a focus ring on a link in the text included, beyond an em on the other three sides.

`.chat-quote-text` keeps its own clamp. It sits inside a block that settles as a whole, so its rhythm is not at stake; moving it to `.u-clamp` is its own item.

The runtime gains about sixty lines, one probe at start in every browser, and nothing per element where the box is trimmed.
