# D107. Stages: seven seeds for a process, each with a fill and an ink

## Context

Collab OS draws its work in a process of six steps and one more for what is closed: idea, planned, scheduled, underway, done, integrated, closed, each in a fixed color. Trellis had three statuses (ok, warn, alert), which say how a thing is, not how far along it is. So the app kept three lists by hand for every status: the color, a darker version for words, and a version for the dark theme. On the owed-cards bench the seven colors were the largest count of raw values in the check (`--st-*`).

Agent Weaver has the same shape under other names (exploring, planning, scheduled, working, done, integrated, closed). Trellis's own status file has it too.

A process's colors are often pale: light blue for an idea, gold for a plan. A kind's seed is kept near the middle of the lightness scale so ink reads on it; a stage's cannot be, because the product's colors are given. Two things in core assumed a seed near the middle: the dark theme lifts every hue by `--color-lift`, which takes a gold at .89 to white; and a hue's own color is not text.

## Options considered

Product names in core (`--seed-idea`). Core is neutral; a product names its steps.

A seed a product names for itself, with roles worked out for it (the open item "a product's own seeds"). CSS cannot make a variable's name from another, so core would have to learn each name; stages are the ordered case two apps already share, and the arithmetic here is what a named seed would use.

An open count. Every stage is a written variable, on the root and on every scope. A fixed number of slots, of which a product uses the first few, is what open can mean; more can be added without breaking anything.

Six stages, with what is closed drawn in the neutral ink. Closed is not a step forward. The owner chose seven, the last for what is closed, so a product can give it a color of its own (Collab OS's warm gray).

Tokens alone, each app setting `--dot-color: var(--stage-3)`. Each app would repeat the same seven rules for the dot and seven for the tag.

Defaults in one hue from pale to full, or in Collab OS's own order of hues. Neighbors in one hue are hard to tell apart as half-cell dots, and a product's order of hues does not belong in the core.

Keeping a lifted color's chroma, as the kinds do. A derived seed is held where that works. A product's stage is not, and a deep blue lifted with its chroma leaves sRGB by half a channel.

## Decision

Chosen with the owner on 2026-11-19: seven stages with the last for what is closed, variants on the dot and the tag, and defaults stepped round the wheel.

- **Seven seeds**, `--seed-stage-1` to `--seed-stage-7`, knobs with no default, read as `var(--seed-stage-n, var(--derived-stage-n))` on the root and on every scope, like every seed that follows the accent (D96).
- **The default** is the accent turned an eighth of the wheel further for each stage, 45° to 315°, so no stage has the accent's own hue; its chroma is .85 of the accent's and no more than sRGB holds; its lightness is the accent's, kept clear of the ink flip. It is a kind's arithmetic with another turn (`follow()`).
- **Three roles for each.**
  - `--stage-n`, the fill: the seed by day. At night the seed lifted by `--color-lift`, but no higher than a derived seed is lifted (.61 plus the lift, .78), and never lowered: a seed already paler than that is unchanged.
  - `--stage-n-ink`: the seed's hue where it reads as text. By day no lighter than .48; at night no darker than .76. A seed already past those ends is its own ink.
  - `--stage-n-tint`: the fill at 14%, a tag's share, mixed with nothing, so it is right on any surface.
- **Chroma moves with lightness** (`shade()` in `math.js`): toward black in step with the lightness, toward white in step with what is left of it. A color inside sRGB stays inside, to within a tenth of a channel at the worst hue. A derived stage is therefore a little quieter at night than a kind beside it.
- **Variants.** `.dot.cc-stage-n` takes the fill. `.dot.cc-ring.cc-stage-n` takes the ink: a ring is a line, and a pale fill would vanish as one (D103 does the same for a quiet ring). `.tag.cc-stage-n` sets the tag's one color to the fill, so its tint, its edge and, solid, its fill and computed ink follow.
- **The fill has no contrast floor.** It is the product's color and may be pale; a mark that must be read alone on the page is a ring, or carries a word. The ink has the text floor.
- `tests/contract.js`: each stage's ink at 4.5:1 on the page and every raised level, both themes, for core, every product layer and every scope; and the same for about 2,300 seeds across sRGB, with the gamut and the cap on the lift. `tests/math.js`: the derived seeds and a scope's pale and deep stages, in the browser against `math.js`, both engines. `tests/layout.js`: the variants.

## Consequences

Collab OS sets seven seeds and drops three lists. Its status names map to numbers where it writes markup; a product layer holds knobs only, so the mapping is the app's, not the layer's.

A product with fewer steps uses the first few. One with more than seven waits for more slots, which adds knobs and breaks nothing.

Seven more seeds cost twenty-eight generated lines on the root and on every scope. `tests/perf.js` does not move.

The other hues (kinds, statuses, the accent) are still lifted with their chroma kept, and a product that sets a pale kind still sees it go to white at night. Moving them to `shade()` and the cap would change core's own dark colors, and is its own decision.

The Figma file gains each stage's fill and ink as colors. The tag component has no stage variants yet.
