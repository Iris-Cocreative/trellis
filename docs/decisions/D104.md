# D104. A row's mark sits on its first line

## Context

A list row lays its parts out with `align-items: flex-start`. Text in a type role is trimmed to its capitals, so the row's parts hang from the top of the first line's capitals. That suits a two-cell face beside a name and a preview. A mark the size of a line does not sit right: a dot hangs from the top of the capitals and reads high, and a face a cell and a half across reaches below the baseline and reads low.

The planning app's owed-cards bench met this three times: an action's sub-action rows, the rows of its Index, and a person's list of what they owe. Each time it restyled the primitive (`.list-item { align-items: center }`) or built a box of its own beside the row's text (`.ok-lead`, one cell tall). Two of the bench's last three restyle counts came from it.

## Options considered

`--list-align` alone, set to `center`. It centers the parts on the row, which is the first line only while the row is one line. A row that wraps to a second line carries its mark down to the middle of the block. Text also rounds its block up to whole cells: one line of `.t-body` is a two-cell box with its capitals in the top half, so a mark centered on that box sits six pixels under the capitals' middle. The bench's rows were `.t-sm` and `.t-xs`, where the error is a pixel.

Centering a mark with no wrapper, from its own size (`margin-block: calc((1cap - var(--dot-size)) / 2)` on a row's dot, face and icon). It needs no markup, and it would move the face in every row there is.

A slot one cell tall, as the bench had it. A cell is a capital only near `.t-sm` and `.t-body`.

Baseline alignment (`align-items: first baseline`). The part with the tallest ascent sets the row's top, so a large mark would push the text off its line.

## Decision

- `--list-align` on `.list`, read by `.list-item` and `.list-end`: `flex-start` by default, so nothing moves. `center` is for a row that is one line and its controls: a face, a name, a button. Its words take `.cap-center` (rule 8), which keeps their box at the capitals, so they center exactly and wrap safely. In a grouped list the body stays stretched for its rule and centers its content instead.
- `.list-mark`, at a row's start or in its end: a flex box `1cap` tall with its content centered. It sits at the row's top, where the first line's capitals are, and what it holds is centered on them and may reach past the box, into the row's padding. The capital is of the mark's own type: with the role of the line beside it (`span.list-mark.t-sm`) it is exact; with no role it takes the type around the row, which is within a pixel or two of the capitals from `.t-xs` to `.t-body`. A role rounds a block up to whole cells; the mark overrides that and keeps its capital.
- At the row's start the mark is a column `--list-mark-w` wide, one cell by default, with the mark at its start (rule 7: a nine-pixel dot and a twelve-pixel column do not differ by an even count, so it is not centered). The row's words then start on a cell line whatever the mark's size, and marks of different sizes share a column. In the row's end the left edge is free, so the mark is as wide as what it holds, at the end.
- A cap-centered line directly in a `.list-body` stretches across the body. `.cap-center` centers itself in its row, and in the body, a column, that had put it in the body's middle.
- `tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and touch: a mark's middle against the middle of its line's capitals, in a one-line row and a wrapped one, at the start and in the end; a centered row's face, words and button on one middle; every row whole cells.

## Consequences

A row never needs `align-items` set from outside. The bench can drop `.ow-row`, `.oc-subs .list-item` and `.ok-lead`.

A mark in a `.list-mark` does not add to the row's height, so it should be no taller than the first line plus the row's padding: two cells in an ordinary row, a cell and a half in a dense one. A larger face stays a direct child of the row and hangs, as before.

A mark wider than a cell reaches into the gap before the words: a list of faces sets `--list-mark-w` to two cells.

`tests/align.js` reads a centered row as one thing, as it does a dense row: its edges are on lines, and its parts center on each other.
