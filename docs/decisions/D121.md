# D121. A row centers its words in the space it takes

## Context

A list row's text hung, as text does everywhere (rule 8, the Hang relation): each block was trimmed to the top of its capitals and its last baseline, and rounded up to whole cells on its own. The row's padding was the same above and below, so what rounding added always landed under the last baseline, once per block. Small type made it worse, and so did a cell that a capital only just passed: at the phone's cell (9.8125px) a one-line `.t-sm` title is a hair taller than a cell in core and Almanac, so it took two.

Found on 2026-11-27 in the planning app, whose rows are small type on a 12px cell: a dense two-line row was 6px from its top to the title's capitals, 15.75px from the title's baseline to the meta line's capitals, and 11.19px from the last baseline to its bottom. A dense row halved its padding and not the gap between its lines. In core's specimen, a one-line row beside a face was 12px over its words and 25.58px under them; the inbox, 12 over and 21.09 under; a dense row on a phone, 4.91 over and 14.52 under.

The owner decided on 2026-11-28: a row centers its text in the space it takes (the Center relation), a dense row halves the gap between its lines, and rows stay whole cells tall. The build session worked out how.

## Options considered

**Round once and center the body, the rest as they are** (`align-self: stretch` and `justify-content: center` on `.list-body`, text in it not rounded). The words center, and a grouped row's rule, drawn from the body, still meets the row's bottom. But a mark and the end hang from the row's top, and the first line now starts lower by an amount CSS can't give them: a `.list-mark` would leave its line (D104), and a time in the end would sit above the title.

**Center the row's line** (`flex-wrap: wrap; align-content: center`). The mark, the words and the end make one flex line and move together, so the first line keeps its mark and its end. But a face taller than the words is in that line too: the line is the face, and the words still hang from its top, 12 over and 25.58 under. And a grouped row's rule, drawn as an outset of the body, no longer reaches the row's bottom.

**Keep each block's rounding and center its ink in its own box** (`align-content: center` on each text block). One line is centered exactly; two lines are off by half the difference of their two roundings, the gap between them grows by both, and the first line moves by an amount a mark can't know.

**Keep the row's height and center in it.** Nothing outside a row would move. But the height it keeps is the sum of each block rounded on its own, which is the fault: on a phone a two-line row keeps a cell of air that only a capital's hair over a cell put there. CSS can't add up roundings it no longer makes, so it would also take a second count in script in every browser.

**Measure in script** (settle.js writes each row's offset). It handles everything, at the cost of a pass in Chromium, where rows now settle in CSS alone, and a frame where rows show unsettled.

**Three tracks (chosen).** A row is a grid of three rows of tracks, `1fr auto 1fr`. The words, the marks and the end share the middle one, so they hang from one line among themselves as before (D104), and its height is the tallest of them. Anything else at the row's start or end, a face or an image, spans all three, at the row's top. The row rounds up to whole cells once; whatever that adds, and whatever a face taller than the words adds, goes to the two outer tracks equally. The columns are the body's, `minmax(0, 1fr)`, with what comes before it placed in columns before (`:has(+ .list-body)`, up to three) and what comes after in columns after, each as wide as it is.

## Decision

- `.list-item` is `display: grid`, rows `1fr auto 1fr`, columns `minmax(0, 1fr)` for the body and `max-content` for the rest, `column-gap: var(--list-gap)`. `.list-body`, `.list-end` and a `.list-mark` at the row's start are in the middle track; any other child spans the three; `.list-actions` stays out of the grid. `--list-align` still sets where the parts meet: `flex-start`, each at the top of the middle track (and a face at the row's top), or `center`, each in its middle.
- A row rounds up to whole cells once: `height: calc-size(fit-content, round(up, size - 1/32px, u))`, or in Safari a count from `settle.js` that holds the row's height. Nothing in a row rounds on its own: text roles, `.pair`, `.cluster`, `.settle`, `.field` and a wrapped `.cap-center`. Layout rounds each outer track up to 1/64px, so the two together may come to 1/32px over a line; that is not a cell more.
- The gap between a row's lines is the stack gap exactly, a cell, and half a cell in a dense list, with its padding.
- A grouped row's rule is the row's own `::after`, placed from the body's column to the row's end (`grid-column: 1 / auto`), a hairline after its bottom as a plain row's is.
- `--settle-n` is registered with no initial value, so a row that has no count yet is the height of its content, not of nothing.

## How it sits with rule 8

Rule 8 and the Hang relation are about text in a flow: each block hangs from a cell line and rounds up to whole cells, so whatever follows starts on a line. A list row is not a flow of blocks; it is one thing, as a dense row already was (rule 6) and a centered row was (D104): its edges are on lines, its padding is whole cells, and inside it the words keep their own rhythm. So the row stands on the grid as a box, by Inset and Outset, and its words are placed in it by Center. Rule 8's text beside a control still centers its capitals on the control, and a label that wraps still hangs, everywhere but inside a row, where the row has already centered the whole. Nothing outside a row changes how it meets a line.

## Consequences

- Measured on the specimen in both engines, core, Quarry and Almanac, at 1440 and on a 393 phone: every one of 276 row measurements (23 rows, twelve pages) has as much space over its words as under them, to 0.02px. Before, the rows that were not already centered differed by 0.13 to 15.06px.
- At a 12px cell no row changes height, and nothing outside a row moves: of 35,340 boxes outside rows (core, Quarry and Almanac at 1440, Quarry on the phone, both engines), none differs in place, size or paint but the typing dots' animation and, once, a button's transition caught mid-way that did not repeat. At the phone's 9.8125px cell, rows whose title's capital is a hair over a cell are a cell shorter in core (14 rows) and Almanac (6), and what follows them moves up by those cells: 7,956 of 17,592 boxes moved, every one by exactly the rows above it or inside it, with nothing else about it changed. Quarry's rows keep their height.
- The words move by a few pixels, down where rounding had left space under them; at 1440, in both engines and the three layers: 1.3 to 3.6px in a two-line row, 6.2 to 7.5px for one line beside a face, 4.3 to 6.6px in the inbox, 0.2 to 1.5px in a dense row. A face stays where it was. A mark stays on its first line's capitals, and the end on the first line.
- A row whose end is taller than its words (a button beside one line, `--list-align: flex-start`) is set by the end: its words hang from the end's top. That row is one line and its controls, and takes `--list-align: center` (D104).
- A dense two-line row is shorter by the half cell its gap gave back, where that crosses a cell.
- A row with more than three children before its body places the fourth over the third. None in core has more than one.
- `tests/layout.js` builds nine kinds of row (one line, two, wrapped, an end, marks, a mark beside two lines, a face beside one line, beside a pair, beside three lines) in a plain, a dense and a grouped list, in a card and in a board, one of each selected, and holds each whole cells, centered to half a pixel, its marks on the first line, its end on the first line, its face at the top and its lines a cell apart, half in a dense list; `tests/align.js` holds every row on every page it opens centered to half a pixel. Both fail on main.

### Rows elsewhere

Other primitives a reader takes for rows, and whether the same fault is theirs:

- `.options-card-item`, `.shell-link`, `.outline-link`: controls. Their label is centered in a whole-cell box already (`align-items: center`), so not this fault. Their label is untrimmed, so it is centered by its line box rather than its capitals; that is a question of its own.
- `.choice`: its words center their capitals on the box (D109). Not this fault.
- `.member-tile.cc-row`: a card, whose words hang from its padding (Inset); a face taller than its words leaves space under them. The same shape, but a card's text hanging is the rule for every surface, so it is left to its own decision. The skill already says to center the body on a tall face.
- A flat chat row (`.chat-thread.cc-flat > .chat-item`): a message in a thread's stack, its row filled on hover. Owned by the conversation block; not changed here.
- A `summary` in `.prose`: its hover ground is half a cell round a summary that hangs and rounds up, so the ground has the space under its words. The same fault, inside prose's rhythm, which `tests/align.js` holds to lines block by block. Not changed here.
- A table in `.prose`: each line is whole cells and its text is untrimmed, centered by half-leading. Not this fault.
