# D95. What scrolls in a card is cut at the card's line

## Context

D66 kept a `.card-scroll` inside the card's block padding, so a classic scrollbar ran on the straight part of the card's edge and the corner never cut its thumb. The cost showed in the specimen's Roles demo: rows were cut a cell inside the card, at the scroller's edge, where a scroll pane standing on its own (Default · thin) cuts at its own edge. The owner asked for the cut at the card's line.

## Options considered

A, at the card's start or end the scroller reaches the card's edge and takes the card's corners, and pads its content by the card's padding, so nothing moves at rest; B, keep D66 and fade the content out over the padding, which still leaves a band where nothing is cut cleanly; C, make the card itself the scroller, which loses the card's outer frame (R3) and its padding for anything that doesn't scroll.

## Decision

A. A `.card-scroll` that is the card's first part reaches the card's top edge, and one that is its last part reaches the bottom edge, each with the card's corners there; its content is padded by `--card-pad-block` at that end. Between other parts of the card it stays where it is. The card is the scroller's height plus nothing at those ends, so a card around a fixed-height scroller is shorter by its block padding.

## Consequences

What scrolls is cut at the card's line. Where scrollbars are classic (Chrome and Edge on Windows with the thick bar), the track runs into the corners and the corner trims the thumb's ends when it is at the top or bottom: the trade D66 avoided, accepted for the cleaner cut. A thin scrollbar, the default, is barely touched. R3 and the README say "at the surface's own edge and corners".
