# D108. A card is one link, and an image fills its frame

## Context

A card or a row that opens something is the commonest thing an app draws, and the first app's code makes a whole card one link 72 times. Trellis had `.list-link` for a row, which covered the row but left a button in the row's end under its cover, and nothing for a card. Wrapping a card in an `<a>` is the shortcut that breaks: a link may not hold another link or a button, and a screen reader reads the whole card as the link's name.

Its cover images were all drawn by hand as `background: var(--img) center / cover` on boxes it made up: a full-bleed card with words on a scrim, a banner fourteen cells tall bleeding to a panel's top and sides, a thumbnail strip three cells wide at a line's start, a photo whose focal point is a quarter of the way down so faces are not cut, and square and round tiles. A background cannot be described, loaded lazily or read, and each box gave back the card's padding and guessed at its corners by itself.

## Options considered

The card as an `<a>`. Nothing inside it can be a control, and its whole text is the link's name.

A script that sends a click anywhere on the card to its link. It needs a runtime, breaks the link's own middle click and context menu, and a press on text has to be told from a selection.

The cover drawn from the card with `:has()`: `.card:has(.card-link:hover)` fills the card and draws the ring. A card that holds a card holds its link too, and no selector can name the nearest card, so an outer card would light with an inner one.

For the image, a background with a variable for its address, as the first app had it. Inline styles may set variables, so it is allowed; but it has no `alt`, no lazy loading and no intrinsic size, and it is not an image to anyone who cannot see it.

`aspect-ratio` for the frame. A width that follows its column with a height from a ratio is a height in pixels that are not whole cells, and what follows it leaves the lines.

## Decision

- **`.card-link`**: the card's title is a link, and its `::before` covers the card, its nearest positioned ancestor. The cover sits a step up (`z-index: 1`) and the card's own controls (links, buttons, fields, labels, summaries, anything with a `tabindex`) are raised a step above it (`position: relative; z-index: 2`, at no specificity, so a primitive's own position wins). A card or a row with a link is isolated, so neither step leaves it.
- **The state layer is on the cover** (D55): hover and press mix the ink into it, selected (`aria-current`, `aria-selected`) adds the accent mark at its start, and the focus ring is the cover's outline, with the card's corner. Drawn on the cover rather than on the card through `:has()`, the state belongs to the one card the link is in.
- **`.list-link`** keeps its row behavior and gains the same steps: its cover goes up one, and a button in the row's end is raised above it.
- **`.media`**: a frame sized in cells, `--media-w` by `--media-h` (no width fills the column; the height is twelve cells), with an `<img>`, `<video>` or `<picture>` inside that fills it by `object-fit: cover` at `--media-focus`. The image is taken out of the flow, so the frame's size is the stylesheet's alone and nothing moves when it loads. An empty frame shows `--media-fill`; `--media-scrim` is drawn over the image by the frame itself, not by a second element. In a card its corners are concentric with the card's (rule 21); `.cc-round` makes it round.
- **Bleeding** is the frame as a direct child of the card: it gives back the card's padding and takes the card's own corner where it meets the card's edge, and square corners where it doesn't. `.cc-bleed` reaches both sides, and the top as the card's first part and the bottom as its last (a banner; in a `.card.stack` the stack's gap parts it from the words). `.cc-bleed-start` and `.cc-bleed-end` are a strip, three cells wide unless `--media-w` says otherwise, as tall as the card, which becomes a row with its body beside the strip. `.cc-fill` is the card's whole ground: at least `--media-h` (eighteen cells) tall, under a scrim to 80% black, with the card's edge dropped and its words in white at the foot (a card of a frame and one body).
- `tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and touch: every point on a card, its corners included, is its link; its button and tag link are above the cover, and a list row's button above the row's; real clicks on each go to the button or the link they should; the focus ring is the cover's outline round the card, from the keyboard, and the link draws none of its own; each image's box is its frame's, by cover at the focal point; a frame of eight by ten cells is that; the banner, the strip and the full bleed meet their card's edges and take its corners where they meet them.

## Consequences

A card that opens something needs no wrapper and no script, and can still hold buttons, tags and a menu. Text under the cover cannot be selected by dragging, which is the usual trade for a card that is a link; a part that must be selectable can take a `tabindex` and so be raised.

The cover covers the link's nearest positioned ancestor: the card, unless the link sits inside a row or another positioned part of it. A card that holds a card puts the inner card under the outer cover; give the outer card no link, or put the inner one beside it.

A bleeding frame must be the card's own child. Inside a stack inside the card it keeps its own corners and stays in the stack.

The words on a full-bleed card are white, as on a `.cover`; secondary and tertiary text roles keep their colors, so a full-bleed card uses the text's own ink or a scope with `data-theme="dark"` for them.

A control there with no fill of its own (a plain or ghost button, a tinted tag, a choice, a switch, a tab, a slider) kept the page's ink on the scrim, near-invisible by day, until 2026-11-24. It reads `--text`, `--text-secondary`, `--text-tertiary` and `--border-strong` as white or a share of it, set on the control itself, so its hover and press (its own ink, mixed) follow; every focus ring on the card but the cover's, which is drawn on the page, is white. Set on the card's body instead, the same four would reach a card, a float or a modal inside it, which have grounds of their own; those, and a scope with a `data-theme`, are left alone. What has a fill (a primary button, a solid or selected tag, a field, a segmented control) reads none of them. The scrim is what makes white read, and it fades toward the card's top: words and controls that reach up there need a `--media-scrim` that does.

`.card-media` stays, a box with the concentric corner and no size; `.media` is the frame to use for an image.
