# D90. Color from one seed: the accent, and nine that follow

## Context

The relational palette (0.10.0) made every role a seed at a lightness, and left a product ten seeds to choose: a neutral, an accent, five kinds and three statuses. Ten colors that must sit together is a designer's job, and a product that set only its accent kept core's other nine, chosen for core's blue.

The specimen's Color section then asked how few colors a palette needs, and answered one: a study (`palette.js`) derived all ten seeds from a chosen accent, with a harmony for the kinds, the statuses at their own hues and the neutral with a trace of a hue. It brought each derived seed inside sRGB and nudged its lightness until its ink read at 4.5:1, in a loop. The owner decided core should derive the same, each seed still settable.

## Options considered

Weighed at the time:

Keeping ten seeds, with the study as a tool that writes them. A product that sets only an accent still gets nine colors chosen for another.

Core derives the nine from the accent, each still settable.

Named while building it, not weighed at the time:

The study's own method in core. `core.css` cannot loop or search: a seed must be an expression of the accent's lightness, chroma and hue. So the two things the loop did are stated in closed form instead: a lightness kept clear of the ink flip, and a chroma limit by hue.

Letting a derived seed leave sRGB and be clipped. The browser's clipped color is not the one named, and its ink was measured against the named one.

A knob for the harmony. Four turns would be four more knobs, and a product that wants another harmony can set the four seeds, which the study writes out.

Keeping the study's order of kinds (person a fifth round, group two fifths). From core's blue that put group on orange and person on pink. The turns are assigned so each kind keeps its character from core's blue: person warm, group teal, event purple, course green.

## Decision

A product chooses one seed, `--seed-accent`. The other nine default to tokens derived from it, `--derived-neutral` to `--derived-alert`, and a product may set any `--seed-*` in its place.

- **Lightness, kept clear of the flip.** A derived seed takes the accent's lightness, but where its ink is white it is no lighter than 0.54, and where its ink is dark no darker than 0.61. The dark theme lifts a seed, so one whose ink is white is also no darker than 0.61 less the lift. With that, ink reads at 4.5:1 on every derived seed, for any accent inside sRGB, in both themes.
- **A kind** is the accent turned around the hue wheel, a fifth of it apart: event 72°, person 144°, course 216°, group 288°. Its chroma is 0.85 of the accent's, and never more than sRGB holds at its hue.
- **The limit** on chroma is the lightness times a number that depends on the hue: straight segments between fourteen hues around the wheel (0.399 at red and from blue to red, down to 0.154 at 225° and 0.19 at 90°), under the real limit of sRGB at every hue for a seed whose ink is white. The hues and numbers are in `tokens.json`.
- **Topic** is the accent 0.045 darker, with its chroma scaled down by as much as its lightness.
- **A status** keeps its own hue (ok 152°, warn 70°, alert 30°), takes the accent's lightness, and 0.9 of its chroma held between 0.1 and 0.17 and under the same limit at that hue.
- **The neutral** is the accent's hue at a chroma of 0.004.

`math.js` says the same in arithmetic: `derived()`, `seeds()`, `follow()`, `clear()` and `chromaLimit()`. The numbers are in `tokens.json`, `color.derive`.

## Consequences

- Breaking: core's own kinds, statuses and neutral move. From the default accent: person `oklch(.54 .141 42.4)`, group `oklch(.54 .092 186.4)`, event `oklch(.54 .141 330.4)`, course `oklch(.54 .12 114.4)`, topic `oklch(.508 .152 258.4)`, ok `oklch(.54 .14 152)`, warn `oklch(.54 .115 70)`, alert `oklch(.54 .149 30)`. The neutral takes the accent's hue, so core's grays turn from warm to cool, at the same trace of chroma.
- A product layer that sets a seed keeps it. One that set only some seeds gets the rest from its own accent, where it had core's.
- A derived seed whose ink is dark (from an accent lighter than the flip) can still sit outside sRGB at blue and red hues, where sRGB holds less chroma as lightness rises. Its ink still reads.
- The limit is segments and not a smooth wave because `cos()` on a channel inside a relative color is not safe: WebKit reads degrees there as radians, and Chromium before 2026 reads the channel as nothing. In `core.css` a segment is its slope times `clamp(0, hue − its start, its length)`, which every engine with relative colors computes. The first build used two cosine waves and failed in CI's Chromium.
- A seed's hue is read between 0 and 360.
- `tests/contract.js` derives the seeds for about 2,000 accents across the wheel and fails if an ink falls under 4.5:1 in either theme, or a white-ink seed leaves sRGB. `tests/math.js` reads the nine seeds from the browser, for core's accent and four others, and checks them against `math.js` in both engines.
- The study now draws core's rule. Its other harmonies and neutrals go past it, and it writes out only the seeds core would not have derived the same.
