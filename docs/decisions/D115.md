# D115. What a button does, what a label quiets, and what a bar lets through

## Context

The membership app's mockups and the planning app each draw, in their own CSS, a destructive button, a label with no kind, a count on an icon button's corner and a translucent bar. The membership app also draws a confirm button, an outline of each kind, and loads a face for code because the family was not a knob. The owner approved building these on 2026-11-24, and left event, course and person buttons to wait for a second app.

The two apps do not draw them alike:

| | The membership app | The planning app |
|---|---|---|
| Destructive | Filled with the status, ink computed; an outline in the status's color | A tint (10% by day, 20% at night) with the label in the status's color, no edge; in the live room a 35% tint |
| A label with no kind | The neutral's tint and edge, secondary ink; also pressed, solid and outlined | A neutral ground, no edge |
| A count on a corner | A wrapper and a badge a quarter cell past the corner | A badge inside the button, 3px past the corner |
| A bar | A color of its own at 85%, blurred 1¼ cells | The page's ground at 90%, blurred |

While a product layer was being drafted, a defect was found: in `.cc-dense` a card's corner is `--r-1` whatever the product's `--shape-card` says.

## Options considered

**Names.** `.cc-destructive` and `.cc-confirm` say what the button does, and a third app would have to learn that a tag's alert is a button's destructive. `.cc-danger` is the same. Core's names for the same two colors are `.cc-alert` and `.cc-ok`, on the tag and the dot, and the membership app had already chosen them. For the label, `.cc-muted` (the membership app) and `.cc-secondary` (the planning app); core has `.cc-quiet` on the badge and the dot. `.cc-outline` is taken by the tag, where it empties the fill and keeps the edge. For the count, `.cc-corner`: a place, as `.cc-left` is a tray's.

**What an outline is drawn in.** The kind's color itself, as both apps do: the accent and the statuses are held at 3:1, as lines, so a label in one can be unreadable (3.1:1 for a product's alert at night). The text's ink with the edge in the color, as a tag does: readable, and not what either app draws. The stages' ink (D107), no lighter than 0.48 by day and no darker than 0.76 at night: it reads at rest, and falls to 4.49:1 for core's confirm when pressed. The same arithmetic a step further, 0.46 and 0.77: at least 4.86:1 in core, both example layers and every scope of the study, pressed, on every raised level.

**The planning app's tint.** An emphasis of its own (`.cc-soft`): a class nobody else asked for. A share in the outline's ground, `--btn-tint`, as a tag has `--tag-fill`: the same button, one variable.

**Outline alone.** Nothing, since a plain button is an outline already. Or the text's ink, edge and label, as the membership app draws it: a class that does nothing is a trap.

**Hover and press on a filled kind.** The shared state layer mixes the control's own ink into its fill (D55), which lowers the contrast of a white label as it does on a primary button: core's confirm, 4.74:1 at rest, is 4.24:1 hovered and 3.81:1 pressed, its accent 4.88, 4.35 and 3.88. Mixing black in for these two kinds would hold 4.5, and a destructive button would then darken where the primary beside it lightens. One state layer is kept; the floor while pressed is 3:1.

**A selected outline.** Solid, as a pressed tag is; but the button's other emphases keep their fill when pressed and change their edge. Its edge, two lines.

**Where the count sits.** On the button's outer corner by a fixed offset, as both apps have it: on a two-cell button it covers the icon. From the button's middle: the badge's inner corner is a quarter cell short of the middle, and at most a cell and a quarter in, which on the sizes both apps draw is a quarter cell past the corner. A wrapper and a sibling, as the membership app has it: an element per button, and a click on the badge misses the button.

**The count past the cell's cap.** At 312% text a badge is a cell larger each way. Growing inward it covers the icon. Growing outward it covers the control beside it, so the button keeps that room, in whole cells, as a margin that is nothing at default sizes.

**The bar.** A share alone (`--shell-nav-ground: 85%`), held at its floor by `max()`: the membership app's ground is not the page's, and a side column, with nothing under it, may be clear. `--shell-nav-bg` as a color, the limit written down and measured. The blur's default as `0px`: a filter of any blur makes the bar the containing block of what is fixed inside it, so the default must be no filter; the variable is empty and `blur()` with it is `none`. Forcing the alpha up with a relative color: a bar nobody could make clear.

**The limit.** Over black and over white, the worst of anything. 75%: `--text-secondary` is 4.46 at rest and 3.60 pressed. 80%: 5.25 and 4.19. 85%: 4.73 pressed, 5.9 at rest, on `--bg` and `--bg-raised`, in core, the example layers and every scope. The focus ring is the accent, held at 3:1 on the page and no more, so over a tenth of something else it can fall under (2.15:1 in the scopes study); the text's ink as the ring does not.

**A dense card's corner.** From `--shape-card` by any rule: core's 1½ cells, Almanac's 2 and Quarry's ¼ are all drawn at 1 today, so a rule that follows the knob redraws Quarry, whose dense cards are rounder than its cards. `min(var(--shape-card), var(--r-1))` is the rule it would be, and it changes a shipped default for every product squarer than a cell. A knob of its own, which nothing sets.

## Decision

**A button's kinds have the status's names.** `.button.cc-alert` is destructive and `.button.cc-ok` confirms, filled with the status, the ink computed from the fill as a solid tag's is (C4).

**`.button.cc-outline` takes the fill away.** Edge and label are one ink, worked out from `--btn-color` so it reads as text: `--btn-color` is the kind's (`.cc-alert`, `.cc-ok`, `.cc-primary`), the text's with none, or a context's own. `--btn-tint` is the share of `--btn-color` in its ground, none unless set; up to a tenth, the label holds 4.5:1, hovered too. On a full bleed it is white (D108). `--btn-bg`, `--btn-ink` and `--btn-border` remain what a context sets to paint a button itself.

**`.tag.cc-quiet`** is the neutral: `--tag-color: var(--text-tertiary)` and `--tag-ink: var(--text-secondary)`, so every emphasis and state of a tag follows.

**`.badge.cc-corner`**, as the child of an `.icon-button`, which names the count in its own label.

**`--shell-nav-bg` and `--shell-nav-blur`** on `.shell-nav`. The limit: at least 85% of `--bg` or `--bg-raised`. Opaque in forced colors and under `prefers-reduced-transparency`.

**`--font-mono` is a knob,** with the value it had.

**`--shape-card-dense` is a knob:** one cell unless set. Whether a dense card should follow `--shape-card` instead is the owner's, since it changes what a shipped layer draws.

## Consequences

What the planning app draws is `.button.cc-alert.cc-outline` with `--btn-tint: 10%; --btn-border: transparent`, and a quiet tag with `--tag-edge: 0%`. Its night tint of 20% and its room's 35% are under 4.5:1 while hovered or pressed; they are its own to choose.

`tools/product.js` holds an outline's ink and a solid quiet tag's at 4.5:1 for every product. `tests/contract.js` holds the rest for core, every layer and scope, and an outline's ink for any color in sRGB. `tests/layout.js` reads the drawn colors in both engines and themes.

On a two-cell button the count stands ¾ of a cell past the corner, over the edge of a control half a cell away. It is not drawn on an avatar: a face does not grow with the reader's text, and the count would cover it.

`.cc-dense .chat-item` has the same fixed corner a dense card had, and is left as it is.
