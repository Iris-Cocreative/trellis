# D111. Past the cap, a float grows with its type

## Context

The cell follows the reader's text size up to a cap (one and a half times the default) and stops; above that only type grows, and a control grows by whole cells so its label never outgrows it. An options card did not. It is `--options-w` wide, 16 cells, and at 312% text (the largest iOS accessibility size) its labels were wider than the card. It used to scroll sideways with nothing a keyboard could reach; the minimal fix made it scroll down and never sideways, and a long label truncated (rule 11). So a reader at the largest text size saw option labels cut off: in the specimen's Set role card, "Remove role" needed 241px of words in a 231px label on a wide screen and 194px on a phone.

Two answers were open: the card stays 16 cells and truncates, or it grows with its type up to the screen's width, as controls grow. The owner left the choice to the build.

## Options considered

Stay 16 cells and truncate. Nothing to build, and the card never covers more of the page than it was designed to. But the reader who most needs the words gets an ellipsis, in a menu, where the label is the whole of what an option says.

Size the card to its content (`width: max-content` or `fit-content`). It would fit its words at any size, but at default sizes it would no longer be 16 cells, its width would not be whole cells, and every product's `--options-w` would stop meaning anything.

Grow by the text size itself (`1rem` over 16px). That grows below the cap too, where the cell already grows with the type, so a card at 140% text would be wider in cells than at 100%, which moves things at sizes that are fine today.

## Decision

**Past the cap, an options card's width grows with its type, in whole cells, up to the screen's width less its margins.** Its width is `--options-w` times how far the type has gone past the cell, rounded up to a whole cell, and no more than the screen less half a cell each side (float.js's margin), rounded down to a cell, and no more than its container. It is never narrower than `--options-w`.

How far the type has gone past the cell is `--u-type` over `--u-base`: `--u-type` is the cell the reader's text size asks for (the rem line the cell's target is made from, now a token of its own), and `--u-base` is that line capped. Below the cap they are the same value, so the factor is exactly 1 and nothing moves at default sizes, nor anywhere up to the cap. At 312% on a wide screen it is about 2.1, so the 16-cell card is 34 cells; on a phone it reaches the screen's width first and stops there, and a label longer than the screen still truncates.

The division of one length by another needs typed arithmetic; where a browser lacks it, `@supports` keeps the card at `--options-w`, as before. The tooltip has no set width and is not changed.

## Consequences

`--u-type` is a new token (read it, don't set it), written by `tools/build-tokens.js`; `--u-base` is now `min(var(--u-type), …)`, the same value.

`tests/layout.js`, in both engines, wide and touch, opens the specimen at 312% text and measures its inline options card and its Roles menu: whole cells, at least 16 of them, inside the screen, no sideways scroll on the card or the page, and no label cut off unless the card has reached the screen or its container. It fails with the factor held at 1. The default width is still checked with the knobs.

A product that sets `--options-w` gets the same growth from its own width. An options card at large text covers more of the page than it does at default sizes; that is what the reader's text size asks for.
