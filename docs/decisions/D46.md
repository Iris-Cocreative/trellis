# D46. Cap the cell at 1.5

## Context

The cell follows the reader's text size. Accessibility sizes on iOS and Android reach about three times normal, which would turn a 40-cell phone into a 13-cell phone.

## Options considered

No cap, or a cap above which only type grows and layouts reflow. The cap.

The number was not weighed. The review session proposed 1.5, and it was accepted as proposed. No other value, such as 1.25 or 2, was tested until the comparison below.

## Decision

The cell follows the reader's text size up to 1.5 times the default and no further. Above that only type grows.

## Consequences

Rule R19. `text-scale-max` in `tokens.json`, `cellScale()` in `math.js`. Controls past the cap needed their own answer (D52), and WebKit's reading of the cell its own fix (D53).

## Compared

Made on 2026-11-24, after the decision, and not weighed at the time. The specimen in three scratch copies that differ only in `text-scale-max` (1.25, 1.5, 2), `tests/align.js` in each, light theme, in Chromium and WebKit: core alone at text of 100, 125, 150, 200, 250 and 312%, and Quarry and Almanac at 200 and 312%, on a 393 and a 320 phone and at 1440 wide. 180 runs. Up to 125% the three are identical.

Core alone, both engines alike. Cells across the screen; a line of body text in the specimen's band, in ems:

| Screen | Text | 1.25 | 1.5 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 393 phone | 100% | 40 cells, 21.9em | the same | the same |
| | 150% | 32 cells, 14.3em | 26 cells, 12.6em | 26 cells, 12.6em |
| | 200% | 32 cells, 10.7em | 26 cells, 9.4em | 20 cells, 9.8em |
| | 312% | 32 cells, 6.9em, **about 1,190 of 1,309 blocks off** | 26 cells, 6.0em | 20 cells, 6.3em |
| 320 phone | 150% | 26 cells, 10.2em | 22 cells, 9.6em | 22 cells, 9.6em |
| | 200% | 26 cells, 7.7em | 22 cells, 7.2em | 16 cells, 7.5em, **scrolls sideways (360)** |
| | 312% | 26 cells, 4.9em, **about 1,200 off** | 22 cells, 4.6em, scrolls sideways by 1px in WebKit | 16 cells, 4.8em, **scrolls sideways (360)** |
| 1440 wide | 100% | 120 cells, frame 100, 45.5em | the same | the same |
| | 150% | 90 cells, frame 72, 43.3em | 80 cells, frame 64, 43.0em | 80 cells, frame 64, 43.0em |
| | 200% | 90 cells, frame 72, 33.1em | 80 cells, frame 64, 32.9em | 65 cells, frame 48, 29.7em |
| | 312% | 90 cells, frame 72, 21.7em | 80 cells, frame 64, 21.5em | 65 cells, frame 48, 19.3em |

- **Lines.** 1.5 and 2 are 0 off in every run. 1.25 is 0 off at 250% and under, and breaks at 312% on a phone with core and with Almanac (not Quarry, whose type is smaller), where type is two and a half times its usual size in cells. A row of the specimen's ratio playground is two cells at least and its type is 2.14 cells tall, so everything below it shifts; with that row made three cells in the scratch copy, WebKit is 0 off again and Chromium still breaks, at a `.choice` row, which is core's. 1.5 meets the `.choice` row at 340% and the playground's at 375%, past the largest text size iOS has (312%). The lower the cap, the sooner type outgrows a row counted in cells.
- **Reflow.** At 393 and 1440 nothing scrolls sideways with any of the three. At 320, the width WCAG's reflow criterion names, 2 scrolls sideways from 200% in both engines and all three layers: the screen is 16 cells, and a part 16 cells wide (the hue wheel, an options card) does not fit inside its gutters. At 312% on a 320 screen a small tag also runs past its card's edge: in Quarry with all three caps, in core by 1px with 1.5, in WebKit.
- **What each costs.** On the 393 phone, from 150% up, 1.25 gives a line of body text 13 to 15% longer than 1.5 and a line in a card 21 to 22% longer, cuts fewer labels short (25 against 31 at 312%) and makes the page 16 to 17% shorter; on the 320 phone 5 to 7%, 12 to 14% and 9 to 12%. On a wide screen it gives 1 to 2%. 2 gives the phone's body line 4 to 5% over 1.5 (a gutter of two cells against three) and takes 2 to 4% from a card's, and takes a tenth from a wide screen's line with a page 16 to 17% longer.

2 is ruled out by reflow. Of the other two only 1.5 keeps everything on its line, and it stays. 1.25 has a case, and it is the phone's: at large text a reader gets a fifth more in a card's line, and what it breaks is two rows that could be made to hold. Whether that is worth a smaller cell (controls, gutters and gaps that stop growing at 125%) is the owner's question, and is not settled here.

Seen on the way: `tests/align.js` at 320 reports an empty `textarea` that scrolls sideways (with 1.5 at 312%, with 2 from 200%) as out of a keyboard's reach. A textarea takes focus by itself; the check looks for a `tabindex` on the scroller or something focusable inside it.
