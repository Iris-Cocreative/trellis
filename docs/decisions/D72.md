# D72. Compositions that paint, case by case

## Context

The definition says a composition arranges and paints nothing. Three did paint: `.list` draws a hairline after each row and a hover ground on a row that links; `.prose` paints its `pre`; `.cover-media` paints a sunken ground and a scrim.

## Options considered

Weighed at the time, on a decision card in the plan: A, the definition gains a clause: a composition may draw its own dividers and a hover or media ground, and nothing else; B, the parts change: a list row's hover becomes a surface, the cover's media is a surface; C, case by case.

## Decision

C, case by case. A composition may draw its own dividers (the list's hairlines). A list row's hover ground and a cover's media are elements treated as surfaces: they paint, the composition holding them doesn't. The `pre` in prose is already a surface.

## Consequences

The anatomy pages say so; no code changes. A composition that wants to paint anything else becomes a surface holding a composition, as before.
