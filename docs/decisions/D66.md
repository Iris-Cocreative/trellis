# D66. A scroller inside a card's corners

## Context

The specimen's scroll demo put a scroller that inherited the card's corner inside a card with no padding, R3's second option ("lets an inner scroller clip"). Where scrollbars are classic (Chrome and Edge on Windows) the track runs the scroller's full height, and the card's 1.5-cell corner cut off the thumb's ends.

## Options considered

Weighed at the time, on a decision card in the plan: A, a `.card-scroll` part that spans the card's sides but stays inside its block padding, which is at least the corner by default, so its scrollbar runs on the straight part of the card's edge; R3 amended to "lets an inner scroller clip, inside the surface's corners"; B, a concentric inner radius on the scroller, as `.card-media` has, which only helps where there is padding; C, hide the scrollbar and draw an indicator, which costs runtime and accessibility.

## Decision

A. `.card-scroll` takes the card's inline padding back as negative margin and pads its own sides by `--card-scroll-pad` (the card's, by default), so its content lines up with the card's and its scrollbar runs on the card's edge. It stays inside the card's block padding; where a product sets the corner larger than that padding, it steps in by the rest, rounded up to whole cells. The demo uses it.

## Consequences

An additive part and variable. R3's wording amended, not its meaning: a scroller still clips itself so the card can keep its outer frame. The specimen's demo is a dense card with a `.card-scroll` inside.
