# D85. A mark is a straight line: the corners it touches are square

## Context

A current link in the shell or the outline keeps a mark of its own beside its fill: a two-line accent edge (D58). The link has half-cell corners, and the mark was an inset shadow, so it bent around them: a colored line curling into a rounded corner at each end. The owner called it out on the specimen as a trope of generated interfaces, and asked that a colored edge never round the corners it touches.

## Options considered

Keeping the curve. It is what an inset shadow on a rounded box draws, and it is the look the owner rejected.

Drawing the mark as its own straight bar inside the rounded box. The box's corner would still clip it, or the bar would stop short of the corner and float.

Squaring the two corners the mark touches. The mark runs the full edge as a straight line, and the far corners keep their radius.

Dropping the radius from the link altogether. The hover and press fill would lose its shape on every link to fix the one that is current.

## Decision

The corners a mark touches are square. A current `.shell-link` and `.outline-link` square their two start corners; in a top bar, where the mark is under the link, the two bottom corners; in the menu tray, the start corners again.

## Consequences

- The rule is general: a new primitive that marks an edge with color squares the corners on that edge.
- A selected row in `.list.cc-group` is not covered: its first and last rows take the group's corners, and squaring them would let the row's fill leave the surface. Open until a design asks.
- A tab's mark is its own line under the tab and was already straight.
