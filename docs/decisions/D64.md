# D64. Hanging rows keep their space below

## Context

In the specimen's comments the second comment's name looked closer to the rule above it than the first comment's text did to the rule below it. A list row pads a cell top and bottom, but hanging text rounds its block up to whole cells, so the space under a row's text is the padding plus up to a cell of slack and the descenders, while above the next row's text it is exactly the padding to the capitals: about 17px under, 12px over, at 1440. The owner also saw the Measure overlay's text guides about two pixels off the x-height and the cap line.

## Options considered

Weighed at the time, on a decision card in the plan: A, accept and document: the hang model working as written, shown in the Measure overlay and on the list's page; B, a standing-row variant, `.list.cc-stand`, whose text stands on whole-cell lines so both sides are near-symmetric, with a looser two-cell line; C, the runtime measures the slack and the row gives back half.

## Decision

Find why the guides looked off first, then A. The text is on its line: in Chrome at 1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2 device pixels per pixel, the capitals' ink lands within one device pixel of the cap line, which is the browser snapping glyphs to the pixel grid. The guides were drawn on the box's edges, correctly, but only at the cap line, while the eye reads lowercase at the x-height, about 3px lower at 15px; the gap above a name read 3px wider than the cell it is. The overlay now draws the x-height too, dashed, for lowercase text, and its legend names both lines. The row spacing stays, and the list's page (A3) and Hang (M2) say why.

## Consequences

No change to core. The specimen's Measure overlay draws a second guide per line of lowercase text, from the font's own cap and x-height read through a trimmed probe. B stays available as a variant if prose-like rows ever need to look symmetric; it would be breaking as a default.
