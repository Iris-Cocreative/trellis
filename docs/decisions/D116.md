# D116. A field's box holds slots, and a card can be a choice

## Context

Two entries in the first app's ledger, and the planning app draws both.

**Something in a field's box.** The membership app sets a password's show button and the `$` of a gift beside the input, in a row, because an overlay would restyle the input's padding. The planning app's library draws a pill with a search icon, a bare input and a clear button that comes when there are words, and its portal panels, pickers and menus draw the same with a hand-made box. Core had one padding for both sides of `.input`, and one box that holds controls: the composer.

**A whole card that is a choice.** The membership app's sign-up asks one of two ways in, each a card with a radio at its head, lit in a hue with a glow when chosen, and the second card holds controls of its own: a segmented control, a slider, a field and a link. The planning app's index picks a kind from a strip of cards, each an icon tile, a name with a count and a line of fact; the chosen card takes a ground and an edge and its tile inverts. Both are buttons with `role="radio"` and a script that moves the state. D109 left a card that is a choice to a kit "until a second app asks".

## Options considered

### The field

**The input keeps its box and the slots lie over it,** its padding set to their widths. Nothing about the input changes, and autofill and focus are its own. The padding is the layout: a unit's width ("kg", "https://", a currency from data) is not known in CSS, so either the app counts it in cells, or the words run under it at large text.

**A flex box with a bare input,** as the composer is. Units of any width lay out by themselves. But the input is only the middle: a click on the icon or the unit lands on the box, so it needs a script or a label (a label round a button puts the button's name in the field's), and the browser's autofill paints the middle and leaves the slots.

**The runtime measures each slot** and writes the padding. It needs the script before the first paint.

**A flex box whose control's own box runs on under the slots** (chosen): padding to its words, and the same out again as a negative margin, the box clipping what is over. The words stay where flex puts them, whatever the slot's width; the distance decides only how far the control reaches under it. A unit is no longer than that distance, so the control always reaches the edge: with the distance a guess and the unit free, a wide face on CI's machine left a strip of "a month" that didn't focus.

**A class for a start slot and one for an end.** The order in the markup already says it, in both directions of reading.

**A wrapper round a whole `.input`.** The app's markup stays; but the wrapper draws the edge and the ground, and an input's variables are set on the input, where its parent can't read them.

### The card

**The label wrapped round the card.** A label may hold no other control, and the membership app's card holds five; its whole text becomes the radio's name.

**`role="radio"` on the card, with a script,** as both apps have it. It gives up the form, the arrows and `required`, and a radio may not hold a slider.

**The state drawn from the card with `:has(:checked)`.** A card that holds a choice card holds its radio too, and no selector names the nearest card: an outer panel would light with a card in it (the reason D108 gave).

**The choice's own label covers the card** (chosen), as a `.card-link` does.

**A `cc-choice` variant of `.card`.** The state is the input's and the cover is the label's; a class on the card would say what the markup in it already does.

## Decision

### `.input.cc-slots`

- The box is a `div.input.cc-slots`: an input in every way it is drawn (height, edge, ground, corner, sizes, density and any context that sets `.input`'s variables), holding a bare **`.input-control`**, an input or a select. What comes before the control stands at the start and what comes after at the end.
- A slot is an **`.icon`** or an **`.input-unit`** (text), drawn and nothing more (`pointer-events: none`), or a **`.button.icon-button`**, which is its own control. Icons and units are in the second ink (`--input-slot-ink`), half a cell from the words; an icon or a unit starts where a plain input's words do, a button is a quarter cell from the edge all round.
- **The whole box is the field.** The control reaches every edge of it, so a click on a unit, an icon or the padding focuses it, with no script, and autofill fills the box. `--input-unit` (6em) is a unit's longest (and half the box), past which it is cut with an ellipsis, and how far the control runs under it. The browser hangs its own suggestions from the control, so beside a short unit they may start a few ems before the box. For an icon and an icon button the distance is exact, so a select's list opens under its box.
- **The box takes the one ring** and the invalid, disabled and read-only states, from its control. A button in it keeps its own ring, drawn inside, since the box clips. On touch the button is as tall as the box allows, and its target is 44px.
- **The browser's own parts stay between the slots:** a number's spinner and a search's clear are in the control's words' room. A search's own clear and Edge's reveal are hidden where a button follows the control, which is the one that clears or shows.
- **A select takes a start slot;** its end is its chevron. **A textarea takes none:** which line a slot is level with, and where its scrollbar goes, have no one answer, and controls round lines of text are the composer.
- Right to left follows from the order and from logical properties. A select's chevron, which was drawn at the right in both directions, over its words, is at the left there, for a plain select too.

### `.card-choice`

- The card's title is **`label.choice.card-choice`**, and its `::before` covers the card, the label's nearest positioned ancestor. A press anywhere on the card is a press on the label, so the native input keeps everything (D109): Space, a radio group's arrows from card to card, the form, `:disabled`, `:user-invalid`. The card's other controls are beside the label, never in it, and are raised above the cover as a link card's are. What describes the choice is an `aria-describedby`.
- **Chosen, the card lights:** an edge two lines thick on the card's own edge (`--card-choice-edge`, the choice's color) and a wash under its words (`--card-choice-fill`, 8% of it). The edge's thickness says it without the color. **Mixed** lights the edge and not the ground. `--card-choice-glow` is a shadow a product may add; none by default. The wash takes contrast from the third ink, which has none to spare on a nested ground, so a lit card that holds no card reads `--text-tertiary` as the second.
- **Invalid** is the alert's edge, from the input or from its `role="radiogroup"`, where ARIA allows `aria-invalid` for radios. **Disabled** sinks the ground and quiets the title; the description stays readable, since it often says why. **Hover and press** are the shared layer on the cover, and **the one ring** is round the card.
- Where a part of the card itself shows the choice (a tile that inverts), the input may be `.u-sr-only`. Everywhere else it is drawn.

### How it differs from `.card-link`

A `.card-link` goes somewhere; a `.card-choice` is a value in a form. The first is an anchor, with a middle click and a place in the tab order of its own; the second is one of a group the arrows move through. A card is one or the other. **Where both are on one card, the choice is the card** and the link is a plain link: no cover, its own click and ring, underlined. So the two covers never lie over each other, and nothing that was a link stops being one.

## Consequences

`:read-only` matches a `div`, so `.input`'s read-only rule leaves one out.

A card that holds a card keeps D108's limit: the outer may not be a choice or a link while the inner is. The handling of a link in a choice card, and the third ink, are for a card that holds no card; an outer panel is not touched by the choices in it.

Text in a choice card can't be selected by dragging, as under a link.

A button with words in a field's box is as wide as its words; the control reaches under it by `--input-unit`, and the rest of it is the button's own.

A number field with a unit at its end has its spinner inside the unit. Apps that want none set `inputmode` on a text input.

Not in the Figma template yet (`docs/figma/README.md`).

`tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and touch, and `tests/contract.js` (text on a lit card in core, every layer and scope, and 48 accents) hold it. Fifty-two mutations of these rules were tried, and each fails a test that names what it broke.
