# D112. A bubble holds typed text, or prose, and both keep their shape

## Context

The membership app's mockups found two things wrong with what a `.chat-item-bubble` holds.

A message typed with Shift+Enter lost its line breaks: the composer shows them, the bubble collapsed them. The app set `white-space: pre-wrap` on the bubble itself, and the planning app does the same wherever it draws a message (comments, the assistant, the live room).

A message rendered from Markdown had no width. A `.prose` is a size container, so it says nothing about its own width to a parent that shrinks to its content, and a bubble does: prose in one, or a bubble that is itself `.prose`, came out two cells of padding round nothing. A wall post collapsed the same way. Only a flat row, whose bubble is as wide as the row, drew it.

## Options considered

**Line breaks.** A modifier (`.cc-lines`): every app that draws messages would add it to every bubble, and forget one. `pre-line`, which keeps breaks and collapses spaces: gentler on markup indented in its source, but a pasted snippet loses its indent, and neither app that asked draws it so. Every bubble, whatever it holds: the line breaks between the tags of a bubble of paragraphs would draw as empty lines.

**Prose.** Stretch every bubble that holds prose to the message's measure: one rule, but apps render every message through Markdown, and a one-word answer would be a bubble 40 cells wide. Stop prose being a container everywhere: its type would no longer answer to its column in an article, which is what prose is for. A wrapper the app adds: the same gap in each app.

## Decision

**A bubble of words and inline marks keeps the line breaks and the spaces it was typed with** (`white-space: pre-wrap`). A bubble that is prose, or that holds a block (a paragraph, a list, a heading, a `br`, one of the message's own parts), sets its own lines, and the spaces between its tags draw nothing. The rule has no more weight than the bubble's class, so a utility or an app's own rule wins.

**In a bubble that shrinks to its content, prose is not a container.** D74 says why: a container's width can't come from what it holds. The bubble takes the width of its words up to the message's measure, and the prose's type answers to the thread, as the words in the bubbles beside it do. Code and an embed have no width of their own to give (a `pre` is a container, an embed is measured from one), so a bubble that holds one takes the message's measure and its prose is a container again. So does a wall post, which has no bubble to shrink. A flat row is unchanged. Code steps out of its column by the product's outset and, in a bubble, no further than the bubble's padding.

## Consequences

A message whose text holds line breaks now shows them; an app that set `white-space` on its bubbles can drop it. An app that turned its line breaks into `br` is unchanged. A page written by hand keeps a bubble's plain text on its tag's line: text on lines of its own between the tags now draws the line breaks round it. None was found in the specimen or in the apps on Trellis.

Prose that had a width before by accident, from something wider beside it in the bubble, is now as wide as the wider of the two, and its headings follow the thread's width rather than the bubble's. `--chat-prose` carries the choice (`normal`, or `inline-size` in a flat row, a wall post and a bubble of code or an embed); it is core's own.

`tests/layout.js`, in both engines, wide and touch: one word of prose, in the bubble, as the bubble and in your own, is the word and a cell each side; long prose, code, an embed, a wall post and a flat row are the message's measure; an embed keeps its shape; code stays inside its bubble, dense too; three typed lines are two lines taller than one; a bubble of paragraphs, of a `br` or of prose draws no extra line. Each fails without its rule.
