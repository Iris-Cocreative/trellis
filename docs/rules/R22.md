# R22 · Padding clears the corner

## Statement

A card's content stays out of its corner's curve. Its padding in each direction is held to the share of its radius that clears the curve: 0.293 of it with the same padding both ways, and less, down to none, as the other side goes further in. Where a padding falls short it grows by whole cells.

## Why

A corner of radius r is a quarter circle, and content's own corner is a point p in from one side and q from the other. The point is inside the curve when (r - p)² + (r - q)² ≤ r², or when either is past the radius. With p = q that is p ≥ r (1 - 1/√2). Trellis sets the two paddings by two knobs, so a product can ask for a tight side on a very round card; the curve says how tight is possible.

## The failure it prevents

A word, a row's mark or an image's corner cut by the curve of the card it is in.

## How it is checked

`tests/layout.js`, both engines, wide and touch: seventeen pairs of paddings on corners from none to five cells, each laid out and its content's corner measured against the circle; the paddings that clear it left exactly as asked; a pill, a bare `0`, a nested card; and a banner, a strip, a full bleed and a scroller meeting the edges of a card whose padding was raised.

## Example

A card with a corner of five cells and one cell of padding lays out two. The same card with one cell at its sides and four above and below keeps them: its content begins where the curve is nearly over.

## Exceptions

A corner drawn past `--card-radius` (one corner set by hand, as an arch) is not seen. A radius counts up to eight cells, since a pill's real corner is half its height, which CSS cannot read; a pill taller than sixteen cells needs its own padding. A radius given as a percentage has no floor.
