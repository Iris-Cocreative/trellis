# R3 · A surface's edge is a plane after its coordinate

## Statement

A surface's edge is a plane after its coordinate, and never changes its size. A card draws its edge as an `::after` frame one line larger: inside the box on the left and top, just outside it on the right and bottom, so each edge lies on its grid line's pixels. A surface that clips would cut that frame off, so it keeps an inset outline or lets an inner scroller clip, inside the surface's corners: `.card-scroll` spans the card's sides and stays inside its block padding, so its scrollbar runs on the straight part of the edge. Controls with a fixed height keep a real border and give it back in their padding.

## Why

Lines are planes: a line occupies the pixels just after its coordinate. A border inside the box would move the content; a border outside on every side would sit on the wrong pixels on two of them.

## The failure it prevents

A card's edge one device pixel beside the grid line it should cover, or content shifted by the border's width.

## How it is checked

`tests/pixel.js` runs in WebKit at 3× (393×852 and 440×956). It paints every card's edge red on black and checks that each edge covers the same device pixels as the grid line it stands on. 60 card edges, 0 off. Cards in a rail or a board are skipped, since the scroller clips them.

## Example

A card at cell 4 draws its left edge on the three device pixels after cell 4's coordinate, the same pixels the grid line uses.

## Exceptions

Floats align to anchors, not the grid. A clipping surface uses an inset outline instead of the outer frame. A scroller inside a card clips itself, inside the card's corners (`.card-scroll`, D66), so the card keeps its outer frame.
