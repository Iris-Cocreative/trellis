# R1 · Edges land on lines

## Statement

Every edge lands on a cell line, or a hairline or a quarter cell off one. A box centered on a line is then even: 14 = 12 + a hairline, 18 = 12 + a quarter cell each side. A box pinned to something else, such as the screen's edge, takes whatever size lands its free edge on a line. The grid fits whatever frames the page: the screen's edges when fitted, the `.frame`'s own edges on a wide screen.

## Why

The cell is the only line on the page. If edges land on it, everything else on the page can be placed by counting cells, and alignment between unrelated parts comes for free.

## The failure it prevents

Edges that sit a few pixels off the lattice. Each one is small, but they add up down a page, and two boxes that should line up visibly don't.

## How it is checked

`tests/align.js` measures the top and left of every box (cards, plates, stacks, rows, tags, buttons, lists, fields and more) against the cell, across the matrix in Chromium and WebKit, and fails anything more than half a pixel off.

## Example

A card's top edge sits on a cell line. Its text, inset one cell, starts on the next line.

## Exceptions

Floats align to their anchor, not the grid (R16). Content inside a scroller is skipped by the test, since the scroller clips it.
