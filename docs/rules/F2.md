# F2 · Time has a ratio too

## Statement

Durations come from one small set (`--dur-*`) and three easings (in, out, spring). Reduced motion collapses them; the rest state is complete on its own.

## Why

Space and type each have one seed. Motion should too, so every transition in a product feels like the same system.

## The failure it prevents

Twenty slightly different durations across one app.

## How it is checked

Review only. In `core.css` every transition and animation uses the tokens: `--dur-out` 150ms, `--dur-in` 200ms, `--dur-in-slow` 250ms, `--dur-tray-out` 300ms, `--dur-tray-in` 350ms, `--dur-skeleton` 1.4s, and `--ease-in`, `--ease-out`, `--ease-spring`. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the durations become 1ms and the skeleton stops.

## Example

The modal enters in `--dur-in` on `--ease-out` and leaves in `--dur-out` on `--ease-in`; a tray moves on `--ease-spring`.

## Exceptions

None.

## Questioned

The set exists; the ratio doesn't. The owner chose that durations become a ratio from a seed, as space and type are (D70), so the rule stays questioned until the action Motion proportions builds it. The six durations are steps of 50ms plus the skeleton today. The easings are named as the tokens are: in, out, spring. No test or lint fails a raw duration yet.
