# R5 · Surfaces step out (Outset)

## Statement

A card's edge steps `--outset` cells (a product knob, 1 by default) out into the space around it, so the text inside a card with a one-cell padding lines up with the text outside. A card with more padding still steps out one cell. A row steps out as a whole only when it holds nothing but cards; a card inside one column of a row stays in its column; nested, a card steps out into its parent's padding. Gutters are at least two cells, so a card that steps out never reaches the screen's edge. `.list` rows and a `pre` in `.prose` step out by the same rule.

## Why

Text is what the eye follows down a page. If a card's text lines up with the paragraph above it, the card reads as part of the column rather than a box dropped into it.

## The failure it prevents

A column of text and cards where every card's text is indented one cell from the text around it.

## How it is checked

`tests/layout.js` checks that a list row steps out one cell while its content stays on the column's edge. `tests/align.js` runs with `outset` on in five matrix runs (outset is off in the others), so cards that step out are measured on the cells in both states.

## Example

A card with one cell of padding in a `.stack`: its edge is one cell left of the paragraph above, and its first line of text starts exactly under that paragraph's.

## Exceptions

Outset is clipped by any ancestor with hidden overflow (a scroll pane, a clipped section), so a card there silently loses its step-out. Give that ancestor `--outset: 0`, or a cell of inline padding. Through 0.9 this rule and its knob were called Hang.
