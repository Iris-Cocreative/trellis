# R8 · Beside a control, text centers its capitals on the control

## Statement

Text beside a control centers its capitals on the control (`.cap-center`). Both are trimmed to cap height, so centering the boxes centers the capitals. A label that wraps hangs from its top line instead, and its block rounds up to whole cells, like any other text.

## Why

A label and the control it names read as one line when their capitals share a center. Without trimming, the text box carries extra space above and below the capitals, and centering the boxes leaves the capitals visibly low or high.

## The failure it prevents

A row label that looks lower than the button or tag beside it.

## How it is checked

`settle.js` counts the lines of every `.cap-center` in every browser and marks one that wraps with `data-trellis-wrap`; core then aligns it to its row's start and rounds it up (`calc-size()`, or `--settle-n` in Safari). `tests/align.js` checks a wrapped one as text, its top and its end on lines, and still skips one that is centered on its control. `tests/layout.js` builds a label that must wrap beside a button and checks that it is marked, hangs from the row's top, is whole cells, and that what follows is on its line; and that a one-line label beside it is not marked and centers on its button.

## Example

In the specimen's tag matrix, "Kind" beside a row of tags: its capitals' middle is the tags' middle.

## Exceptions

When a matrix row stacks in a narrow container, the label above its tags is ordinary text again and stands on a line.

## History

The rule first assumed one line. At 312% text a row label in the specimen's tag matrix wrapped to two lines, 3.35 cells tall, and everything after it went off the lines, because cap-centered text was not rounded to whole cells. The owner chose that a wrapped label hangs from its top line (D67), and the runtime, core and both tests now carry it.
